# UniPathway tuition: six progress-check workshops

**Status:** Original formative teaching; educator review required. These are cross-grade diagnostic and reteaching workshops, not board mocks or qualification assessments. Versions A (Grades 8–10) and B (Grades 11–12) are options within each workshop, not separate lessons. Use the accessible route first for a new learner and allow written, oral or typed reasoning as appropriate. No real personal data or laboratory work is required.

**Common 40-minute plan:** 5 minutes independent attempt; 10 minutes tutor modelling; 10 minutes paired explanation; 10 minutes independent transfer; 5 minutes exit check. Keep individual results private. Use wrong answers to identify the next teaching step rather than making a placement decision from this one session.

## UP-TU-REV-MA — Estimation and checking a result

**Goal:** Estimate before calculating, then verify using a second method.

**Tutor model:** A £40 item reduced by 15% costs £34: 10% is £4, 5% is £2, subtract £6. Check directly: £40 × 0.85 = £34. Keep pounds and percentages distinct.

**A practice:** A school club has 24 members and grows by 25%. Predict whether the new total is above 28, calculate it, and check by multiplication. **Key:** 25% is one quarter, so add 6 to get 30; 24 × 1.25 = 30. The estimate is above 28.

**B practice:** A curve has height f(x) = 2x + 1 on 0 ≤ x ≤ 3. Estimate using endpoints, then find the exact area by geometry and integration. **Key:** the area is between 3 and 21 square units; trapezium area is 3(1 + 7)/2 = 12; [x² + x] from 0 to 3 = 12. Using just the final height would give the upper bound, not the exact area.

**Exit:** Give one independent check of the result, not merely the same calculation repeated. If learners confuse a bound with an answer, draw upper/lower rectangles or percentage bars.

## UP-TU-REV-PH — Units, graphs and claims

**Goal:** Read units on both axes and avoid drawing conclusions that the data do not support.

**Tutor model:** A cart travels 6 m in 3 s at a steady rate. The slope of distance against time is 2 m/s. A horizontal section means distance is unchanged, not that time has stopped.

**A practice:** A cart’s recorded positions along one straight line are 0 m, 4 m, 8 m and 8 m at 0 s, 2 s, 4 s and 6 s. Find the average speed over the whole recorded journey if it moves forward without backtracking; explain the last interval. **Key:** 8 ÷ 6 ≈ 1.33 m/s; no change in position is recorded over the last interval. Without intermediate observations, the points alone do not establish every moment of motion.

**B practice:** A synthetic velocity–time graph goes linearly from 2 m/s to 8 m/s over 3 s, then remains at 8 m/s for 2 s. Find acceleration in both phases and displacement. **Key:** +2 m/s², then 0 m/s²; displacement (2 + 8) × 3/2 + 8 × 2 = 31 m. Slope is acceleration; area is displacement.

**Exit:** Explain what changes if the horizontal axis is relabelled from seconds to milliseconds while the physical event stays the same. Convert before calculating; physical speed or acceleration does not change.

## UP-TU-REV-CH — What a measurement actually tells us

**Goal:** Separate an observation from a claim about conservation, identity or equilibrium. All figures are fictional.

**Tutor model:** If a closed container and its contents have a combined mass of 120.0 g before a physical change and 120.0 g afterwards, the reported total mass is unchanged at the recorded precision. This does not tell us the identity of every component.

**A practice:** An open container plus contents reads 75.0 g at the start and 73.5 g later. A learner says 1.5 g of matter was destroyed. Evaluate and suggest a comparison using supplied data, not an experiment. **Key:** 1.5 g is the decrease in the measured system. Transfer out of an open system is possible; the data do not show destruction. Compare equivalent synthetic closed-system readings and note measurement uncertainty.

**B practice:** In a fictional closed model, at three successive measurements species X is 0.20 mol/L and species Y is 0.40 mol/L. Are equal concentrations required at equilibrium? Can constant readings alone prove a dynamic process? **Key:** no and no. Equal forward/reverse rates, not equal concentrations, define dynamic equilibrium in an appropriate reversible system. Stable readings are consistent with equilibrium, not proof of the mechanism.

**Exit:** Write one warranted conclusion and one thing the readings cannot show. If these are confused, underline the measurement and box the inference.

## UP-TU-REV-BI — Patterns need a fair comparison

**Goal:** Read biological data and distinguish evidence of an effect from a good explanation.

**Tutor model:** In a fictional class dataset, Group A’s seedlings grow 2, 3 and 4 cm (mean 3 cm) and Group B’s grow 1, 2 and 3 cm (mean 2 cm). A difference in means is descriptive; it does not identify a cause if water and light both varied.

**A practice:** A learner says larger seedlings prove a particular light level is always best. Identify two limitations and one improvement to the comparison. **Key:** small sample and two changed variables are enough to limit the claim; use comparable groups that differ only in the factor being investigated, and collect repeated measurements.

**B practice:** A fictional membrane-transport study gives mean uptake units 12, 12, 13 under condition A and 4, 5, 6 under condition B, with no energy-use data. Calculate both means and evaluate the claim “B proves all transport is passive.” **Key:** A = 37/3 ≈ 12.3; B = 5. Different measured uptake does not establish a transport mechanism or justify “all”. Seek evidence distinguishing available mechanisms and consider spread and sample design.

**Exit:** Change “proves” to suitable evidence wording and name a check that would strengthen the conclusion.

## UP-TU-REV-EN — Edit a claim without losing its meaning

**Goal:** Support a precise claim and revise an overstatement.

**Source passage (original):** “The library recorded 42 visits in the first week of later opening and 55 in the second. Several visitors said the later hour helped them after work. Staff did not record how many other visitors could have come at the old time.”

**A practice:** Write a two-sentence summary containing the figures and one limitation. **Key:** Visits rose from 42 to 55 over the two weeks of later opening. Some visitors found it helpful, but the record cannot establish how many depended on the change. Reward faithful paraphrase and distinction between some and all.

**B practice:** Write a recommendation to the library committee with one caveat and a measurable next step. **Model:** Keep the later hour for a four-week pilot because use rose from 42 to 55 visits between the two observed weeks and some visitors reported a benefit. These weeks do not establish that later opening caused the rise. Compare visits in the final hour across the pilot and earlier comparable weeks and collect responses from visitors who could not use the old schedule.

**Exit:** Replace “Everyone needs later hours” with a narrower claim supported by the passage. Discuss the difference between visits and unique visitors.

## UP-TU-REV-CS — Test scope, boundary and recovery

**Goal:** Distinguish a correct-looking result from a tested decision rule. The exercise uses a fictional school-club setting and generic pseudocode only.

**Tutor model:** A room allows 1–24 participants inclusive. An admission check using n > 0 AND n ≤ 24 accepts 1 and 24 and rejects 0 and 25. An input type check must take place before the comparison if non-numeric input is possible.

**A practice:** For a permitted range of 2–12 whole numbers inclusive, choose two accepted and two rejected boundary tests and explain the expected outputs. **Key:** 2 and 12 accepted; 1 and 13 rejected. A text input such as “many” should return a clear message rather than being silently treated as zero.

**B practice:** Searching a list for an identifier must return a valid zero-based index or a separate not-found value. Test ["A", "B"] with target "A", target "C" and an empty list. Explain why returning zero for absence is wrong. **Key:** found at index 0; not found; not found. Zero is a valid location, so use a distinct result such as -1 or a named absence value. Present this as data validation practice, not approved syntax for a specific paper.

**Exit:** Describe one test that catches a boundary error and one that catches an error in failure handling.
