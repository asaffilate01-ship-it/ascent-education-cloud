# UniPathway scale wave 2: vocational micro-lessons

Original staff-review drafts. Suggested complexity only: L2–L6 labels are internal teaching placements, not qualification equivalence, awarding-body alignment or accreditation. All names, organisations, dates and figures are fictional. Health and social care examples are classroom simulations, not clinical, safeguarding or legal protocols. Never use actual learner, financial or care records in these exercises.

Each 25–35 minute lesson can run as retrieval (3 minutes), model (7), practice (12), feedback and reflection (5–10). The keys identify the reasoning to reward as well as the result.

## W2-VOC-01 · Turn a complaint into a service action

**Business, suggested L2. Objective:** distinguish an issue, an agreed action and an unverified promise. **Starter:** explain the difference between acknowledging a concern and resolving it.

**Worked example:** Fictional customer A says a workshop confirmation arrived after the joining deadline. A helper logs the reported failure and confirms that the workshop team will check the dispatch time by 3 pm. No refund or new place has been approved. Use three fields: “Report: confirmation arrived after deadline”; “Action/owner: workshop team checks dispatch”; “Next update: 3 pm”. Acknowledge the inconvenience without guessing its cause.

**Practice:** (1) A room booking shows the wrong date; the booking officer can compare the confirmation with the request by noon. Write the three fields. (2) Identify and replace the unsupported promise in “Your fee will definitely be refunded by noon.”

**Key:** (1) Wrong date reported; booking officer checks original request and confirmation; update by noon. (2) Refund is unsupported. “I’ll give you an update by noon after the booking officer has checked the record.” Do not mark resolved on acknowledgement. **Exit:** add a status that accurately describes the case now: “under review.”

## W2-VOC-02 · Compare contribution under a bottleneck

**Business, suggested L4. Objective:** rank feasible work by contribution per constrained resource, then check demand and unused capacity. **Starter:** subtract a £9 variable cost from a £24 selling price.

**Worked example:** A fictional print workshop has 12 machine-hours. A poster batch yields £48 contribution and takes 3 hours; a booklet batch yields £30 and takes 1.5 hours. Rates are £16/hour and £20/hour. With demand capped at 4 booklet batches, make those first (6 hours, £120), then 2 poster batches (6 hours, £96). Total £216. This ranking works for this divisible-hours example with whole batches that fit; packing leftovers can change the best mix elsewhere.

**Practice:** With 9 hours available, task X earns £45 in 3 hours per batch and task Y earns £32 in 2 hours; Y demand is at most 3 batches and X demand is unlimited. (1) Rank and propose a mix. (2) Check X-only and the best combinations for 0, 1, 2 or 3 Y batches.

**Key:** Starter £15. X £15/hr, Y £16/hr. With 0, 1, 2, 3 Y batches, best totals are £135, £122, £109 and £141 respectively; optimum is 3 Y and 1 X using all 9 hours. **Exit:** name one omitted consideration: setup times, demand accuracy or quality.

## W2-VOC-03 · Find the critical path for a service rollout

**Business, suggested L6. Objective:** map dependencies, calculate earliest completion and distinguish schedule slack from spare staff capacity. **Starter:** name one reason two tasks may not be allowed to start together.

**Worked example:** A fictional training firm must first agree its timetable (A, 2 days). Instructor briefing (B, 3 days) and venue preparation (C, 5 days) can then run in parallel. The joint rehearsal (D, 2 days) starts only after both finish. From day 0, A finishes day 2, B day 5 and C day 7; D finishes day 9. A–C–D is critical. B can slip by up to two days without moving D, assuming independent resources. Adding people to B does not shorten the project.

**Practice:** (1) Equipment setup E (4 days) now starts after A and is also required before D. What is the critical path and E's slack? (2) If C finishes two days early, when does D finish? (3) If B and E instead need the same person and cannot overlap, then with that early C finish, when is the earliest D can finish?

**Key:** A–C–D remains critical, E finishes day 6 with one day of slack. With early C and independent resources, E finishes day 6 and D day 8. With a shared person, B and E take 7 sequential days from day 2, so D finishes day 11. **Exit:** a task can sit off the critical path and still become critical after a resource or dependency changes.

## W2-VOC-04 · Match a credit note to an invoice

**Accounting, suggested L2. Objective:** calculate a customer's outstanding balance after a part-return and distinguish a credit note from cash repayment. **Starter:** calculate 6 × £8.

**Worked example:** A fictional shop invoices a customer £72 for 9 trays at £8 each. The customer returns 2 trays before paying. A correctly linked £16 credit note reduces the receivable to £56. No bank receipt or payment has occurred. Check the customer, invoice reference, returned quantity and amount before applying the credit. Use tax-free fictional examples for this exercise only.

**Practice:** (1) Invoice I-302 is for 10 folders at £6; customer B returns 3. Calculate the credit and balance. (2) If B later pays £25, calculate the outstanding amount. (3) A note for the exact credit amount instead references I-320. What should staff do?

**Key:** Starter £48. Credit £18, balance £42; after £25 receipt, £17 remains. Do not apply a reference-mismatched note by amount alone; verify and correct the reference using the approved process. **Exit:** Does issuing the note alone move money? No.

## W2-VOC-05 · Correct an accrual across reporting periods

**Accounting, suggested L3. Objective:** recognise a period expense before the invoice arrives and clear the related accrual without double-counting. **Starter:** identify which period used a service delivered in March and invoiced in April.

**Worked example:** A fictional workshop uses £600 of cleaning in March, estimated reliably; the invoice arrives in April for £620. At March close debit cleaning expense £600, credit accrued liabilities £600. In April, when invoiced, debit accrued liabilities £600, debit cleaning expense £20, credit trade payables £620, assuming March is closed and the immaterial estimate difference is treated in April for this classroom example. Paying later debits trade payables and credits bank; it is not a second expense.

**Practice:** March electricity accrual was £460; the April invoice is £440. Under the same classroom assumptions, record the close, invoice and later full payment.

**Key:** March Dr electricity £460/Cr accrual £460. On invoice Dr accrual £460/Cr trade payables £440/Cr electricity £20. On payment Dr trade payables £440/Cr bank £440. Net expense across the two periods is £440. **Exit:** ignoring taxes, name the figure misstated if the accrual is not cleared when invoiced: liability and expense are both overstated under ordinary duplicate posting.

## W2-VOC-06 · Separate price and usage variances

**Accounting, suggested L5. Objective:** decompose actual input-cost variance against allowed output and propose evidence-based follow-up. **Starter:** explain why more output can increase total cost without indicating inefficiency.

**Worked example:** A fictional studio made 200 kits. Standard input is 2 m per kit at £3/m, so allowed quantity is 400 m and allowed cost £1,200. It purchased and used 420 m at £2.90/m. Price variance on actual quantity = 420 × (£2.90 − £3.00) = £42 favourable. Usage variance at standard price = (420 − 400) × £3 = £60 adverse. Net £18 adverse; actual cost £1,218. A cheaper supplier may not explain waste without evidence.

**Practice:** A later run produces 180 kits under the same standard, purchasing and consuming 350 m at £3.20/m. (1) Calculate the two variances and net; reconcile to actual cost. (2) Suggest a check before attributing the price variance to purchasing performance.

**Key:** Allowed 360 m costing £1,080; price £70 adverse; usage £30 favourable; net £40 adverse; actual £1,120. Check like-for-like grade, freight treatment, supplier terms or temporary requirements. **Exit:** do not calculate usage against 400 m when actual production was 180 kits.

## W2-VOC-07 · Make a non-sensitive handover actionable

**Health and social care, suggested L2. Objective:** hand over a routine classroom service action using a fact, action owner and time without sharing unnecessary details. **Starter:** give one reason a recipient needs a clear deadline.

**Worked example:** In a fictional community activity session, the accessible large-print programme is missing from the activity desk. “At 10:10 the activity desk had no large-print programmes. Session host to print five from the approved template before 10:25 and confirm to reception.” This names the resource issue without naming participants. It does not claim nobody can attend or assign blame.

**Practice:** (1) The entrance sign still shows last week's room, and the site assistant can correct it before the 2 pm session. Write a brief handover. (2) Remove unnecessary information from “The sign is wrong; send the entire attendee list and attendance histories to the maintenance group.”

**Key:** Record observed sign and correct location if verified; site assistant changes sign before 2 pm and confirms. No attendee list or history is needed to correct a sign. **Exit:** why add a receipt or confirmation step? So the sender knows whether the routine action was completed.

## W2-VOC-08 · Apply minimum-necessary information sharing in a simulation

**Health and social care, suggested L4. Objective:** identify the smallest suitable set of fictional information for a routine purpose and distinguish unknown authority from permission. **Starter:** identify who needs to receive a community venue's updated room number.

**Worked example:** An authorised session coordinator needs a headcount and the requested number of large-print packs. The volunteer has participant names, personal notes, phone contacts and aggregate counts. Share the verified aggregate counts with the authorised coordinator via the agreed channel. If the coordinator's role or the channel is unclear, pause and ask the designated supervisor rather than guessing. Real urgent or safeguarding situations require the applicable procedures; this activity does not simulate them.

**Practice:** An unexpected email signed “External partner” asks for attendance histories and personal notes, claiming it needs them to set up chairs. Give (1) a proportionate response and (2) the evidence to record.

**Key:** Verify identity/authority through established contacts; do not send requested records. For the stated purpose, an aggregate chair count may suffice if approved and sent securely. Record requested purpose, decision, verified role or unresolved status, data actually shared, approver and channel if relevant. **Exit:** a plausible signature is not proof of identity or permission.

## W2-VOC-09 · Design a continuity plan for a non-clinical community service

**Health and social care, suggested L6. Objective:** evaluate competing continuity options and define escalation, verification and residual-risk checks. **Starter:** distinguish a service output from whether participants could access it.

**Worked example:** A fictional community guidance session loses its venue two hours before opening. Options: accessible alternate venue with half the seating; reschedule with a confirmed larger room; or move online where connectivity is unknown. Before choosing, identify the service owner, critical access requirements, capacity, usable notification routes and the number of people likely to be unable to participate. A short accessible venue session plus additional session may protect continuity only if capacity, staffing and notifications are verified.

**Practice:** Twelve anonymous fictional bookings; alternate venue seats 7, with capacity for a second session only if a second facilitator is confirmed; 4 participants cannot reliably join online. Propose a decision and trigger, and specify how to check who was not reached without publishing identities.

**Key:** Do not promise 12 places from verified capacity 7. Confirm facilitator and two slots before proceeding, or reschedule with verified capacity; online-only excludes at least 4 without an alternative. Use private approved notification and reconciliation of verified acknowledgements, not a public list. Record owner, fallback, deadline and missed-contact count. **Exit:** specify one residual risk: changing venue may impose new travel barriers.

## W2-VOC-10 · Validate a data import without silently losing rows

**Technology, suggested L3. Objective:** write checks for required fields and duplicates, and reconcile accepted plus rejected row counts. **Starter:** distinguish an empty field from zero.

**Worked example:** In a fictional equipment import, asset IDs must be nonempty and unique, and quantity must be an integer zero or above. Rows A01/2, A02/0, blank/3, A03/−1 and A01/1 yield two valid rows and three rejects if the agreed rule accepts the first valid occurrence of each ID. Preserve rejected row number and reason; do not silently coerce or overwrite. The zero is valid; blank ID is not.

**Practice:** Under the same rule import B01/1, B02/0, B02/2, B03/1.5, B04/blank, B05/3. List accepted IDs, rejection reasons and reconciliation.

**Key:** Accept B01, B02 first occurrence and B05. Reject second B02 as duplicate, B03 as not integer and B04 as missing quantity. 6 input = 3 accepted + 3 rejected. **Exit:** a retry is not permission to overwrite; discuss quarantine or a run ID, depending on the import design.

## W2-VOC-11 · Make dashboard freshness visible

**Technology, suggested L5. Objective:** distinguish a legitimate zero, stale evidence and a missing result, and define a freshness contract. **Starter:** ask how a caller can tell “no bookings” from “could not check”.

**Worked example:** A fictional resource dashboard refreshes every 5 minutes. For this exercise, data 10 minutes old or less is fresh. At 10:20, Campus A returns count 0 as of 10:17; show “0, updated 10:17.” Campus B has a last successful count 8 from 10:02; show “last known 8 at 10:02, stale,” or suppress the numeric value if the decision is material. Campus C has no successful response; display “Unavailable,” not zero. Define timestamp timezone and what counts as success.

**Practice:** At 11:00 classify count 4 as of 10:50, count 0 as of 10:49 and a failed first response. Propose a test for malformed successful responses.

**Key:** Fresh at the inclusive 10-minute boundary; stale zero, not evidence of no current bookings; unavailable. Test a 200 response with a missing count or impossible timestamp and verify it displays unavailable or verified last-known stale data rather than zero. **Exit:** explain why a request completion time cannot replace source observation time.

## W2-VOC-12 · Design an auditable backfill for a reporting field

**Technology, suggested L6. Objective:** specify a resumable historical data migration, define invariants and decide when to stop. **Starter:** describe why repeating a batch should not create a different result.

**Worked example:** A fictional room scheduler adds a duration in minutes. Recompute only rows with two valid timestamps where end is at or after start; quarantine reason-coded exceptions without silently converting to zero. A sample of 100 rows gives 96 calculated, 3 missing end times and 1 negative interval. Require 100 = 96 + 4, independent checks of a sample, and zero overwrites of concurrent live edits. Use deterministic batches and store the batch ID and record version for audit and safe retries.

**Practice:** Batch of 50 gives 45 verified writes, 3 invalid inputs and 2 records changed since snapshot. (1) Reconcile and choose treatment for changed records. (2) Propose a measurable abort condition. (3) Describe a rollback safeguard.

**Key:** 50 = 45 written + 3 quarantined + 2 deferred for re-read and revalidation; never overwrite newer edits. Stop on unexplained discrepancy or any detected overwrite of an intervening write. Use owned/versioned changes to revert only this migration's writes where no later edit occurred; preserve durable audit and exception records. **Exit:** a second pass should skip already-correct records, not create duplicate reporting entries.
