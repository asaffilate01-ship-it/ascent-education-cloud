# ACCA practice micro-lessons · wave 2

Original fictional formative practice for staff review. The paper labels below are for the current route; confirm the learner's specification, variant and sitting before release. These are not ACCA assessment materials, a complete syllabus, a qualification, or evidence of professional experience.

## W2-ACC-01 · Reconciling the till and cash book

**Audience and objective:** Foundations in Accountancy · FA1. Separate cash movements from card payments and explain a till discrepancy.

**Worked example:** A till opens with £60. The day's cash receipts total £190, card receipts £125 and cash refunds £15. Expected cash is £60 + £190 − £15 = £235. A count of £232 means a £3 shortage. Retain the count record and investigate instead of changing sales to force a match.

**Practice:** Opening cash £45; receipts £170 cash and £210 card; refund £12 in cash. Counted cash £205. Reconcile and name one check.

**Answer key:** Expected cash £203; £2 surplus. Check the cash-refund record and count again with a witness. Do not add card receipts.

## W2-ACC-02 · Checking a weighted average unit cost

**Audience and objective:** Foundations in Accountancy · MA1. Divide total costs by total units instead of averaging purchase prices.

**Worked example:** Order A brings 20 units at £3; order B brings 30 units at £4. Total cost is £180 across 50 units, so average cost is £3.60. The simple mean £3.50 is wrong because the quantities differ.

**Practice:** Find the weighted average for 15 packs at £6 and 35 packs at £8. Explain why it is closer to £8.

**Answer key:** (£90 + £280) ÷ 50 = £7.40. The larger order had the higher price, so it pulls the weighted mean toward £8.

## W2-ACC-03 · Finding an amount charged to the wrong period

**Audience and objective:** Foundations in Accountancy · FA2. Adjust a prepaid cost so only consumed benefit is in the current period.

**Worked example:** A £960 insurance premium paid on 1 October covers eight months. At 31 December, three months (£360) are expense and five months (£600) remain prepaid. If all £960 was originally expensed, debit prepayments and credit insurance expense £600.

**Practice:** A £1,500 service fee paid on 1 July covers ten months and was charged to expense. Year-end is 30 September. Calculate the charge for the current year and write the adjustment.

**Answer key:** £450 expense; £1,050 prepayment. Debit prepayments £1,050; credit service expense £1,050.

## W2-ACC-04 · Selecting a pricing floor for a short-run order

**Audience and objective:** Applied Knowledge · MA / FIA · FMA. Identify incremental cost for an order when existing fixed costs do not change.

**Worked example:** A workshop with idle capacity is offered an order for 60 units. Materials cost £4 and labour £3 per unit; order-specific setup is £90 and allocated rent £120 is unchanged. Incremental cost is £510, or £8.50 per unit, before profit and other constraints.

**Practice:** Find the floor for 40 units at £5 material and £2 labour each, with £60 extra packaging in total and £100 unchanged allocated rent. Would £8 per unit cover the stated incremental cost?

**Answer key:** £340, or £8.50 per unit. £8 raises £320 and falls short by £20. Commercial approval should also consider capacity, price effects and terms.

## W2-ACC-05 · Distinguishing a collecting agent from a customer

**Audience and objective:** Applied Knowledge · FA / FIA · FFA. Post money collected for another party separately from your own fee income.

**Worked example:** A festival ticket desk collects £900 on behalf of organisers and earns a contractual £90 service fee withheld from collections. If the desk is acting as agent, recognise £900 bank and an initial £900 liability to organisers, then reduce the liability by £90 and recognise £90 fee income. Pay the £810 remaining liability when due.

**Practice:** An agency collects £650 and retains a fixed £40 fee. What amounts go to its own revenue and to the event organiser? How would recording £650 as sales mislead?

**Answer key:** £40 fee revenue and £610 payable to the organiser after taking the fee. £650 sales would overstate the agency's own revenue by £610 under the scenario assumptions.

## W2-ACC-06 · Keeping a residual-income project in view

**Audience and objective:** Applied Skills · PM. Compare ROI and residual income when appraising a divisional project.

**Worked example:** A division earns £42,000 on £200,000 invested, with a 12% capital charge. ROI is 21% and residual income £18,000. A further £50,000 investment earning £8,000 gives 16% ROI and £2,000 incremental residual income. Divisional ROI would fall to 20%, but residual income rises to £20,000.

**Practice:** An alternative requires £40,000 and earns £5,600 per year. At the same capital charge, find the project's ROI and incremental residual income. What incentive problem remains?

**Answer key:** 14% ROI and £800 incremental residual income. A manager rewarded only for protecting the division's 21% ROI might reject a project earning above the specified charge. Check risk and comparability before recommending it.

## W2-ACC-07 · Building a consolidation adjustment for unsold stock

**Audience and objective:** Applied Skills · FR. Remove an unrealised intragroup profit still included in consolidated inventory.

**Worked example:** A wholly owned subsidiary buys goods from its parent for £20,000; they cost the parent £16,000. A quarter remains unsold outside the group. Of £4,000 transfer profit, £1,000 remains unrealised, so reduce consolidated inventory and group profit by £1,000, ignoring tax.

**Practice:** A parent sells goods to its wholly owned subsidiary for £36,000; original cost £24,000. One third remains unsold. Find the unrealised profit and write the direction of the consolidation adjustment.

**Answer key:** £4,000. Reduce inventory and group profit by that amount. This is an adjustment for consolidation, not a change to either company's invoices.

## W2-ACC-08 · Selecting a procedure for an omitted-liability risk

**Audience and objective:** Applied Skills · AA. Design a test starting from external or operational evidence rather than only the recorded ledger.

**Worked example:** The accounts close on 31 December. Several invoices are received in January for goods received in late December. Start from January supplier invoices and match them to dated goods-received records and December payables or accruals. Investigate unrecorded December obligations.

**Practice:** A December maintenance visit is invoiced and paid in January. Describe evidence to inspect and the main assertion at risk.

**Answer key:** Inspect the contract, service completion record, invoice and payment, then check whether the obligation belonged in December and was recognised there. Completeness of liabilities is the main risk, with cut-off relevant.

## W2-ACC-09 · Calculating the cost of skipping a prompt-payment discount

**Audience and objective:** Applied Skills · FM. Compare the simple annualised cost of deferred payment against a financing alternative.

**Worked example:** Pay a £1,000 invoice at day 10 for £980 or at day 40 for £1,000. Skipping the discount costs £20 on £980 for 30 days. Simple annualisation: £20 ÷ £980 × 365 ÷ 30 ≈ 24.8%. It is not a compounded annual rate.

**Practice:** Terms offer 1% off at day 15 or full payment at day 45. Find the simple annualised cost of foregoing the discount and compare it to short-term funding at 8% on a consistent basis.

**Answer key:** £1 ÷ £99 × 365 ÷ 30 ≈ 12.3%. An 8% rate appears cheaper before fees and limits; confirm liquidity, drawdown fees and whether both rates are comparable.

## W2-ACC-10 · Deciding who can accept a launch risk

**Audience and objective:** Strategic Professional · SBL. Assign named ownership and define the decision boundary for a project exception.

**Worked example:** A company is replacing a supplier portal. Accessibility checks were not completed. The delivery lead owns remediation but has no authority to waive a board-set accessibility policy. The project sponsor records the exception, convenes the proper decision body and pauses expansion pending evidence or an authorised mitigation.

**Practice:** The same team cannot reconcile 2% of migrated purchase records. Draft a three-part escalation with affected scope, proposed mitigation and decision owner.

**Answer key:** Example: identify the 2% unmatched records and potentially affected payment/reporting processes; isolate them and independently reconcile before importing; send the evidence and release recommendation to the documented authority rather than letting the migration operator accept the risk.

## W2-ACC-11 · Measuring exchange movements on an unpaid invoice

**Audience and objective:** Strategic Professional · SBR. Distinguish initial recognition, reporting-date remeasurement and settlement of a monetary payable.

**Worked example:** A GBP-functional entity buys supplies on credit for €10,000 at £0.85 per €1. Record £8,500 initially. Closing rate £0.88 gives a £8,800 liability and a £300 exchange loss; settlement at £0.86 results in a £200 exchange gain relative to the year-end liability. Assume no hedge.

**Practice:** A €6,000 payable is initially at £0.87 per €1, closing rate £0.84 and payment rate £0.89 after year-end. Calculate the initially recognised amount, closing payable and each period's exchange difference.

**Answer key:** Initial liability £5,220, closing liability £5,040, first-period exchange gain £180. Settlement cash £5,340, second-period exchange loss £300. Cumulative exchange loss £120.

## W2-ACC-12 · Finding a launch decision's probability threshold

**Audience and objective:** Strategic Professional · AFM. Calculate expected present value and identify the probability at which it breaks even.

**Worked example:** At decision date, a project has NPV +£150,000 if demand is high and −£90,000 otherwise. With a 50% high-demand estimate, expected NPV is £30,000. Break-even is 90 ÷ (150 + 90) = 37.5%. This is a probability threshold, not a rate of return.

**Practice:** A second project has NPVs +£200,000 and −£80,000. Calculate its break-even probability and expected NPV at a 25% success estimate. Give one reason not to treat expected NPV as a guarantee.

**Answer key:** Break-even 80 ÷ 280 ≈ 28.6%. Expected NPV at 25% is £50,000 − £60,000 = −£10,000. A one-off outcome is not the average; the estimated probability and financing capacity for the downside should be assessed.
