# UniPathway practical language lessons: original draft batch
Prepared 19 November 2026. Six lessons. Instructor-led, printable and suitable for adaptation to an LMS. Draft for qualified language-teacher review before publication. These are original teaching materials, not official examination questions, accredited courses or immigration advice. CEFR labels describe intended learning demand, not certification. No lesson guarantees a test score, visa, admission or permission to work.

## Programme boundaries and route signposting
Before recommending an examination, ask learners to identify the exact destination, purpose, receiving institution and application route. Refer them to the authority or a qualified adviser for individual eligibility. Do not ask learners to upload identity documents to an exercise. Use fictional details in class.

- **English:** IELTS Academic, General Training and UKVI Life Skills are distinct products. Life Skills assesses speaking and listening; a four-skill IELTS preparation class is not automatically appropriate for a family-route requirement. UK family-route English requirements vary by application stage and exemptions. Check the current route and accepted evidence before booking, not a course advertisement. Sources: [IELTS test types](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types), [GOV.UK family visa English](https://www.gov.uk/uk-family-visa/knowledge-of-english).
- **German:** basic German evidence can be required for spousal reunification, with exceptions depending on circumstances. Do not advertise A1 as universally required or sufficient for a visa. This class develops everyday A1 communication, not interview scripts to misrepresent circumstances. Source: [BAMF explanation of basic German for spousal reunification](https://www.bamf.de/SharedDocs/Anlagen/DE/MigrationAufenthalt/Ehegattennachzug/ehegattennachzug-en.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=5). The leaflet is older; recheck current consular instructions before advice or booking.
- **French:** joining a French spouse, family reunification with a foreign resident and later residence/citizenship procedures must not be treated as the same route. This beginner lesson is everyday integration preparation; it makes no universal pre-entry test claim. Source: [France-Visas: family of a French national](https://www.france-visas.gouv.fr/famille-de-francais).
- **Italian:** the official A2 language-test guidance concerns the EU long-term residence permit. Do not relabel that requirement as an automatic initial spouse-visa requirement. This A1 lesson is a foundation, not evidence of A2 achievement. Source: [Italian Interior Ministry: language test](https://www.interno.gov.it/it/temi/immigrazione-e-asilo/modalita-dingresso/test-conoscenza-lingua-italiana).
- **Spanish:** family reunification residence/visa procedures are distinct from nationality and its examination arrangements. This lesson is everyday A1 communication and does not claim a universal Spanish test requirement for family entry. Source: [Spanish Ministry: family reunification residence](https://ciudadaniaexterior.inclusion.gob.es/en/web/migraciones/w/autorizacion-de-residencia-temporal-por-reagrupacion-familiar).

References checked for these distinctions on preparation date. Recheck before each cohort; do not turn these notes into an individual eligibility assessment.

## Shared teaching and assessment approach
Use fictional adults and addresses; learners can decline family disclosures. Provide printed transcripts after listening, captions for recorded versions and extra processing time. Do not grade accent against a native-speaker ideal. Assess whether the message is intelligible and the task is completed. Instructor reads scripts naturally, then repeats; there are no audio files in this batch. Reading a transcript is not independent listening evidence.
For beginner role-plays, score four criteria 0–2 each: task details communicated, appropriate questions, intelligibility, and clarification/confirmation. 0 = not yet demonstrated; 1 = with support; 2 = independently. A score of 6/8 with all essential details correct suggests readiness for the next classroom task only, not an examination pass or CEFR award. Give one strength, one correction and a second attempt.

# Lesson 1 — English: finding and confirming study information
**Track:** IELTS foundation, approximately B1–B2; useful across Academic/General listening and speaking preparation. Not a Life Skills mock. **Duration:** 70 minutes. **Materials:** this worksheet, timer, paper.

## Outcomes
Learners distinguish a corrected detail from the original detail; record names, dates, times and prices accurately; give a developed spoken answer with a reason and example; write a clear information request. The short email task is classroom communication practice, not IELTS Academic Writing Task 1.

## Language toolbox
- “Could you confirm the start date?”
- “Does the fee include the workbook?”
- “I heard Tuesday. Did you say Thursday?”
- “I prefer evening classes because … For example, …”
Correction signals: **actually, sorry, instead, not … but …**. Do not choose the first number automatically.

## Instructor sequence
1. Warm-up, 5 min: learners explain when and why they study. Model a two-sentence answer.
2. Vocabulary, 8 min: match enrolment, deposit, timetable and workbook with registration, initial part-payment, schedule and practice book. Clarify that these exercise terms are not a real offer.
3. Listening, 12 min: let learners read the questions for one minute. Read the script twice without showing it. Allow comparison between readings. Reveal transcript afterward and underline corrections.
4. Reading, 10 min: complete the notice questions independently; require evidence for each answer.
5. Speaking, 15 min: pairs take turns asking the prompts. Partner asks one follow-up; instructor gives feedback on development, not invented band scores.
6. Writing, 15 min: draft and exchange an 80–100-word email; check that all three requested points are present.
7. Exit check, 5 min: learner explains one corrected listening detail and asks a clarification question.

## Listening: learner worksheet
Complete each answer with **NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER**.
1. The evening course starts on ______ November.
2. Weekly classes take place on ______.
3. Each class starts at ______ p.m.
4. The full course fee is £______.
5. Learners must buy their own ______.
6. The classroom is in the ______ building.

### Instructor script (original fictional dialogue)
Adviser: Good afternoon. How can I help?
Learner: I am interested in the evening study-skills course. Does it start on the twelfth of November?
Adviser: That was the old date. It now begins on the nineteenth of November.
Learner: And are the classes on Tuesdays?
Adviser: No, Thursdays. The afternoon group meets on Tuesdays, but the evening group meets every Thursday.
Learner: At six?
Adviser: At six thirty, and each class lasts ninety minutes.
Learner: I saw a fee of eighty pounds.
Adviser: Eighty pounds is the deposit. The full course is one hundred and twenty pounds. You also need to buy your own workbook; it is not included.
Learner: Is it in the main building?
Adviser: It has moved to the library building. The entrance is next to the café.

## Reading: fictional notice
**Riverside Study Centre — evening learners**
The reception desk closes at 6 p.m. The evening class begins at 6:30 p.m. Learners arriving after that time should use the library entrance. A tutor will meet new learners there ten minutes before the first class. Bring a notebook. Workbooks can be bought from the centre shop, but the shop accepts card payments only. The centre does not provide parking. Bicycles may be left in the covered rack behind the library.

Mark each statement TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN using only this notice.
1. Reception stays open until the evening class begins.
2. A tutor will meet new learners before their first class.
3. Workbooks cost ten pounds.
4. Cash can be used at the centre shop.
5. There is a covered place for bicycles.

## Speaking prompts
- When do you prefer to study, and why?
- Describe a useful skill you have learned. What helped you learn it?
- Is studying with another person always better than studying alone? Explain one advantage and one limitation.
Sample developed answer: “I usually study in the evening because the house is quieter then. For example, I can practise listening for twenty minutes after dinner without interruptions. However, I review difficult vocabulary in the morning when I feel more alert.” Do not memorise this as a personal story.

## Writing task
Write an 80–100-word email to the fictional centre: introduce your study goal, ask whether beginners can join, and request confirmation of workbook availability. Do not include real contact or immigration details.
Model: “Dear Study Centre Team, I am interested in your evening study-skills course because I want to organise my learning more effectively. I have not taken a similar course before. Could you tell me whether the course is suitable for beginners? I would also like to check whether workbooks will be available in the centre shop before the first lesson. I understand that the workbook is not included in the course fee. Please let me know if I need to reserve a copy in advance. Thank you for your help. Kind regards, Sara.”

## Answers and feedback
Listening: 1 **19 / nineteenth**; 2 **Thursdays / Thursday**; 3 **6:30 / six thirty**; 4 **120**; 5 **workbook**; 6 **library**. Accept equivalent formatting within the stated limit. Reading: 1 FALSE, 2 TRUE, 3 NOT GIVEN, 4 FALSE, 5 TRUE. “Not given” means the notice does not establish the answer; it does not mean false.
Writing feedback: underline the three required points; correct question word order (“Could you tell me whether…”). Speaking: note one clear reason/example and one place where clarification would help. Homework: record a 60-second answer about a genuine learning habit, then transcribe and improve it; recording is optional.

# Lesson 2 — German A1: introducing yourself and arranging an appointment
**Duration:** 60 minutes. **Use:** everyday settlement/family-life communication; not visa eligibility or interview coaching.

## Outcomes and language
Introduce a fictional name and town, ask for an appointment, understand a day/time and request repetition.
- Guten Tag. Ich heiße Sara. — Hello. My name is Sara.
- Ich wohne in Jhelum. — I live in Jhelum.
- Ich möchte einen Termin. — I would like an appointment.
- Am Dienstag um zehn Uhr. — On Tuesday at ten o’clock.
- Können Sie das bitte wiederholen? — Could you repeat that, please?
- Bitte sprechen Sie langsam. — Please speak slowly.
- Vielen Dank. Auf Wiedersehen. — Thank you. Goodbye.
Days: Montag, Dienstag, Mittwoch, Donnerstag, Freitag. Numbers: neun (9), zehn (10), elf (11), zwölf (12).
**Notice:** formal **Sie** is capitalised. With these verbs, use **ich heiße / ich wohne / ich möchte**. Use **am** for a day and **um** for a clock time. Start with whole-hour times; do not introduce ambiguous half-hour expressions in this lesson.

## Instructor sequence
0–8 min: model greeting/name/town and invite fictional identities. 8–18: teach phrase chunks; practise the German w in *wohne* as a v-like sound. 18–28: read the dialogue twice for listening. 28–38: sentence-building. 38–53: role-play and swap roles. 53–60: independent exit exchange and feedback. Learners with literacy support may use picture/time cards.

## Listen: script for instructor
A: Guten Tag. Ich heiße Sara Khan. Ich möchte einen Termin.
B: Guten Tag, Frau Khan. Geht es am Montag um neun Uhr?
A: Am Montag kann ich nicht. Geht es am Dienstag?
B: Ja, am Dienstag um zehn Uhr.
A: Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?
B: Am Dienstag um zehn Uhr.
A: Danke. Am Dienstag um zehn Uhr. Auf Wiedersehen.
B: Auf Wiedersehen.
Questions: (1) What is the surname? (2) Which day is rejected? (3) What day is agreed? (4) What time? (5) Which German phrase requests repetition?

## Learner practice
A. Fill in: **heiße / wohne / einen / um / am**.
1. Ich ___ Sara. 2. Ich ___ in Berlin. 3. Ich möchte ___ Termin. 4. ___ Mittwoch. 5. ___ elf Uhr.
B. Put in order: **Sie / bitte / Können / wiederholen / das / ?**
C. Receptionist card: Monday 9:00 is available, but caller cannot attend; offer Wednesday 11:00. Caller card: give fictional name, decline Monday, ask about Wednesday, repeat the agreed time and close politely. Change the name and day on the second attempt rather than memorising the script.
D. Write three lines confirming fictional name, town and appointment.

## Answers and model
Listening: Khan; Monday; Tuesday; 10:00; “Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?”
A: 1 heiße, 2 wohne, 3 einen, 4 Am, 5 Um. B: “Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?”
Writing model: “Ich heiße Ali Khan. Ich wohne in Berlin. Mein Termin ist am Mittwoch um elf Uhr.”
Exit check: instructor changes the appointment once; learner must confirm the final day/time. Apply shared 8-point rubric; require correct agreed day/time for task completion. Homework: make five fictional appointment cards and practise with a partner. No personal visa narrative is required.

# Lesson 3 — French A1: asking for a class and checking a timetable
**Duration:** 60 minutes. **Use:** practical integration and study communication; no claim that this satisfies an immigration requirement.

## Outcomes and language
Ask about a beginner class, understand weekday/time, distinguish morning/evening and request clarification.
- Bonjour, je voudrais des informations. — Hello, I would like some information.
- Je cherche un cours de français pour débutants. — I am looking for a French course for beginners.
- Le cours est quel jour ? — What day is the class?
- À quelle heure ? — At what time?
- Le mardi à dix heures. — On Tuesdays at ten o’clock.
- Vous pouvez répéter, s’il vous plaît ? — Can you repeat, please?
- Merci beaucoup. — Thank you very much.
Days: lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi. *Le matin* = in the morning; *le soir* = in the evening. **Le mardi** describes a recurring Tuesday schedule. **À** introduces a time. In *je voudrais*, final s is normally silent; model natural phrasing without requiring imitation of one accent.

## Instructor sequence
0–8 min: show a fictional timetable and identify needs. 8–18: model the phrases, contrasting *mardi* and *mercredi*. 18–30: read dialogue twice; second time pause after complete utterances. 30–40: information-gap timetable. 40–52: pairs arrange an appropriate class. 52–60: written confirmation and exit assessment.

## Listening script
A: Bonjour, je cherche un cours de français pour débutants.
B: Nous avons un cours le mardi à dix heures et un cours le jeudi à dix-huit heures.
A: Je travaille le matin. Le jeudi, c’est possible. À quelle heure ?
B: À dix-huit heures.
A: Vous pouvez répéter, s’il vous plaît ?
B: Le jeudi à dix-huit heures, en salle quatre.
A: Merci. Le jeudi à dix-huit heures, en salle quatre.
Questions: 1. Is this a beginner or advanced class? 2. What morning class is offered? 3. Which class does the learner choose? 4. What room? 5. Why is the morning class unsuitable?

## Learner practice
A. Complete with **voudrais / quelle / jeudi / répéter**: “Je ___ des informations.” “À ___ heure ?” “Le ___ à dix-huit heures.” “Vous pouvez ___ ?”
B. Learner A sees: Monday 10:00, room 2; Wednesday 18:00, room 5. Learner B cannot attend Monday and must ask for the day, time and room without seeing A’s sheet. Write the agreed details. Swap with Tuesday 09:00/Friday 17:00 and a different unavailable day.
C. Write a short message confirming the Wednesday class and asking whether it is for beginners.

## Answers and model
Listening: beginners; Tuesday at 10:00; Thursday at 18:00; room 4; the learner works in the morning.
A: voudrais, quelle, jeudi, répéter.
B: Wednesday, 18:00, room 5.
C model: “Bonjour, je voudrais confirmer mon cours : le mercredi à dix-huit heures, en salle cinq. Est-ce un cours pour débutants ? Merci.”
Exit: a new card says Friday 17:00, room 3. Learner asks two questions and repeats all three details. Mark task details and clarification with shared rubric, not accent conformity. Homework: write a fictional weekly timetable and describe three entries aloud.

# Lesson 4 — Italian A1: buying a ticket and checking the departure
**Duration:** 60 minutes. **Use:** everyday travel confidence; foundational language, not A2 residence-test certification.

## Outcomes and language
Request a ticket, state destination, ask departure time and platform, and confirm price before paying in a simulation.
- Buongiorno. Vorrei un biglietto per Roma. — Hello. I would like a ticket to Rome.
- Solo andata o andata e ritorno? — One-way or return?
- Solo andata, per favore. — One-way, please.
- A che ora parte il treno? — What time does the train leave?
- Da quale binario? — From which platform?
- Quanto costa? — How much does it cost?
- Può ripetere, per favore? — Can you repeat, please?
- Grazie. Arrivederci. — Thank you. Goodbye.
Numbers: due (2), tre (3), quattro (4), dieci (10), dodici (12), venti (20), trenta (30). **Alle dieci** = at ten; **alle dieci e trenta** = at ten thirty. *Vorrei* is a useful polite request chunk; full conditional conjugation is not needed today.

## Instructor sequence
0–10 min: picture-based ticket types and destination names. 10–20: model question/answer chunks; practise stress in *binario*. 20–30: read dialogue twice. 30–40: match printed tickets to spoken details. 40–53: ticket-office role-play with price confirmation. 53–60: exit task and feedback. All tickets and prices are invented, not travel information.

## Listening script
A: Buongiorno. Vorrei un biglietto per Roma.
B: Solo andata o andata e ritorno?
A: Solo andata. Quanto costa?
B: Dodici euro.
A: A che ora parte il treno?
B: Alle dieci e trenta, dal binario tre.
A: Può ripetere, per favore?
B: Alle dieci e trenta. Binario tre.
A: Va bene. Un biglietto di sola andata per Roma, dodici euro. Grazie.
Questions: destination? ticket type? price? departure? platform?

## Learner practice
A. Match questions to answers:
1. Quanto costa? 2. A che ora parte? 3. Da quale binario?
a. Dal binario quattro. b. Venti euro. c. Alle dodici.
B. Fill in **un / per / costa / ripetere**: “Vorrei ___ biglietto ___ Milano.” “Quanto ___?” “Può ___?”
C. Traveller: ask for a one-way ticket to Milano; ask price, time and platform; confirm before pretend payment. Clerk: €20, 12:00, platform 4. On attempt two use Roma, €12, 10:30, platform 3. No money changes hands.
D. Write a note with destination, departure, platform and price.

## Answers and model
Listening: Roma; one-way; €12; 10:30; platform 3. A: 1b, 2c, 3a. B: un, per, costa, ripetere.
Note model: “Milano. Partenza alle dodici. Binario quattro. Prezzo: venti euro.”
Exit: instructor deliberately repeats the wrong platform; learner asks again and confirms the final correct one. Score shared rubric and check all four travel details. Homework: create three fictional ticket cards and practise asking questions; do not use this exercise to book real travel.

# Lesson 5 — Spanish A1: registering interest in a community class
**Duration:** 60 minutes. **Use:** daily-life communication and language foundations; not citizenship-test preparation or a universal spouse-visa requirement.

## Outcomes and language
Introduce a fictional name, ask about an evening class, state availability, request slower speech and confirm a meeting room.
- Buenos días. Me llamo Sara. — Good morning. My name is Sara.
- Quiero información sobre el curso de español. — I would like information about the Spanish course.
- ¿Qué días hay clase? — What days are there classes?
- ¿A qué hora empieza? — What time does it start?
- Trabajo por la mañana. — I work in the morning.
- ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor? — Can you speak more slowly, please?
- ¿En qué aula? — In which classroom?
- Muchas gracias. — Thank you very much.
Days: lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes. **Los martes** = on Tuesdays. **A las seis de la tarde** = at six in the afternoon/evening (18:00 in this task). *Me llamo* introduces your name; do not add *es* after it. Spanish h is silent in *hora*; accept intelligible regional pronunciation.

## Instructor sequence
0–8 min: establish fictional identities. 8–18: teach weekday/time chunks. 18–30: two readings of dialogue with questions. 30–40: build questions and check written accents/punctuation. 40–53: information-gap role-play. 53–60: short written confirmation and exit performance. Avoid requesting real addresses, relationship history or immigration details.

## Listening script
A: Buenos días. Me llamo Sara. Quiero información sobre el curso de español.
B: Hay clase los lunes a las diez de la mañana y los martes a las seis de la tarde.
A: Trabajo por la mañana. Prefiero los martes. ¿En qué aula?
B: En el aula dos.
A: ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor?
B: Los martes, a las seis de la tarde, en el aula dos.
A: Perfecto. Los martes a las seis, en el aula dos. Muchas gracias.
Questions: 1. What is the learner’s name? 2. Which morning class is offered? 3. What class does she prefer? 4. What room? 5. What clarification does she request?

## Learner practice
A. Correct the error: “Me llamo es Ali.”
B. Reorder: **hora / empieza / qué / A / ¿ / ?**
C. Fill in **martes / seis / dos**: “Los ___, a las ___ de la tarde, en el aula ___.”
D. Staff card: Wednesday 10:00, room 1; Friday 18:00, room 3. Learner card: unavailable in mornings; ask day, time and room, request slower speech once, and confirm the evening class. Swap roles with different invented details.
E. Write a two-sentence confirmation of the Friday class, then ask whether you need a notebook. Useful new phrase: “¿Necesito un cuaderno?”

## Answers and model
Listening: Sara; Mondays at 10:00; Tuesdays at 18:00; room 2; slower speech.
A: “Me llamo Ali.” B: “¿A qué hora empieza?” C: martes, seis, dos.
D: Friday, 18:00, room 3.
E model: “Confirmo mi clase: los viernes a las seis de la tarde, en el aula tres. ¿Necesito un cuaderno?”
Exit: learner independently introduces a fictional identity, asks two questions and confirms the final day/time/room. Apply shared rubric. Homework: practise a different availability scenario and write four questions rather than memorising answers.

# Lesson 6 — English A1: arranging an appointment (Life Skills classroom practice)

**Track:** Beginner speaking and listening for family and relocation contexts. An original classroom exercise, not a Life Skills mock or a statement about which evidence any learner must provide. **Duration:** 45 minutes.

## Outcomes and language
Ask for an available day or time, hear a correction, confirm the revised booking and give one reason for a preference. Model: “Is Tuesday possible?”, “Could you repeat the time, please?”, “So that is Wednesday at ten?”, “I prefer the morning because I work in the afternoon.”

## Instructor sequence
5 min: warm up with days and times; 8 min: model rising intonation for a confirmation question; 10 min: read the dialogue twice, then ask learners to compare what they heard; 15 min: role-play with a new schedule and switch roles; 7 min: feedback and a second attempt. Learners may use calendar symbols rather than writing. Do not show the transcript until after listening.

## Listening and pair practice
Reception: “Good morning. How can I help?”
Caller: “I’d like to make an appointment. Is Tuesday at ten possible?”
Reception: “Tuesday is full. I have Wednesday at ten, or Friday at two.”
Caller: “Wednesday, please. Could you repeat the time?”
Reception: “Ten in the morning. Come to room three.”
Caller: “Wednesday at ten, room three. Thank you.”

Ask aloud: Which day was full? What other days were offered? Which day, time and room did the caller confirm? Retell the final arrangement to a partner without looking. In the role-play, the first choice is unavailable. The receptionist offers two alternatives; the caller asks for clarification, chooses one and confirms it. Switch roles and change all details.

## Answers and feedback
Tuesday was full; Wednesday and Friday were offered; the booking was Wednesday at 10 a.m. in room 3. Model variation: “Is Monday at nine possible?” “No, but I have Thursday at eleven or Friday at four.” “Could you repeat the Thursday time? … Thursday at eleven, please.” Check accurate details, one clear question, one confirmation and an intelligible response. Retry with different times if a detail was missed. Speaking and listening are the focus; a written answer sheet is optional scaffolding, not the assessment.
