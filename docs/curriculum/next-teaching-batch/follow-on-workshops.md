### UP-ACC-L3-02: Depreciation, disposal and the cash-versus-profit distinction

**Duration:** 55 minutes. **Builds on:** UP-ACC-L3-01 accrual accounting and UP-ACC-L2-01 double entry. **Materials:** calculator and paper or blank spreadsheet.

**Outcomes:** calculate straight-line depreciation; maintain a cost/accumulated-depreciation schedule; record disposal; explain why sale proceeds differ from disposal profit.

#### Learn (15 minutes)

Equipment used over several periods is not normally treated as if its whole purchase cost belongs to one month's operations. Depreciation allocates depreciable cost over the useful life. It is not a cash reserve and is not an attempt to track the asset's precise market price.

For straight-line depreciation with unchanged estimates:

Annual depreciation = (cost − estimated residual value) / useful life.

Carrying amount = cost − accumulated depreciation, ignoring impairment for this exercise. Begin depreciation when the asset is available for use. Use the stated time convention consistently; real reporting policies and changes in estimates need review.

On disposal, remove both original cost and accumulated depreciation. Compare net proceeds with carrying amount to find gain or loss. Receiving sale proceeds increases cash, but only the gain or loss enters profit or loss for the disposal. An asset register should retain purchase evidence, location, useful-life assessment, depreciation and disposal authorisation.

#### Model (5 minutes)

A printer costs CU4,000, has residual value CU400 and a three-year life. Annual depreciation is (4,000 − 400)/3 = CU1,200. After one full year its carrying amount is CU2,800. The entry is Dr Depreciation expense 1,200; Cr Accumulated depreciation 1,200. Bank is unaffected by this year-end entry.

#### Practice (20 minutes; 10 marks)

A fictional learning studio buys equipment for CU9,600 on 1 January Year 1 and makes it available for use immediately. Expected life: four years; residual value CU1,600. Estimates remain unchanged. The studio uses straight-line depreciation and sells the equipment for CU6,200 on 31 December Year 2 after recording two complete years' depreciation. No selling costs or impairment.

1. Calculate annual depreciation and carrying amount after each year (3 marks).
2. Record Year 2 depreciation and the disposal using balanced journals (4 marks).
3. Calculate the disposal gain/loss and distinguish it from cash received (2 marks).
4. Name one record needed to substantiate the disposal (1 mark).

#### Worked answer (10 minutes)

Annual depreciation = (9,600 − 1,600)/4 = **2,000**. Year 1 accumulated depreciation is 2,000; carrying amount **7,600**. Year 2 accumulated depreciation is 4,000; carrying amount **5,600**.

Year 2: Dr Depreciation expense 2,000; Cr Accumulated depreciation 2,000.

A combined disposal entry is Dr Bank 6,200; Dr Accumulated depreciation 4,000; Cr Equipment cost 9,600; Cr Disposal gain 600. Debits and credits both total 10,200.

Disposal gain = 6,200 − 5,600 = **600**. The cash receipt is 6,200, not 600; the remaining 5,600 recovers the asset's carrying amount. Evidence might include an authorised disposal record, sale invoice and matching bank receipt. A bank receipt alone may not identify which asset was sold.

**Exit check (5 minutes):** Does recording depreciation reduce bank? No. Does depreciation stop merely because equipment is temporarily unused? Not automatically; that requires consideration of the applicable accounting policy and facts, not an assumption that no use means no depreciation.

### UP-ACCA-BT-01: Internal controls and an ethical response to pressure

**Paper:** Business and Technology (BT/FBT). **Duration:** 50 minutes. **Prerequisites:** roles in a small business and basic purchase documents.

**Outcomes:** connect a control weakness to its risk; distinguish preventive and detective controls; propose segregation of duties; respond professionally to pressure without inventing accusations.

#### Learn (15 minutes)

A control is useful when it addresses a specific risk and leaves evidence of performance. Preventive controls act before an error or improper transaction completes. Detective controls identify problems after they occur. Neither guarantees the absence of error or fraud.

Separating supplier creation, invoice checking and payment authorisation makes it harder for one person to create and conceal an improper payment. A small team may need compensating independent owner review. Access should match duties, and staff should use individually accountable accounts. A checklist is not a control if nobody checks it or records the result.

Ethical pressure can threaten objective judgement. A threatened personal disadvantage is an intimidation concern; a personal financial incentive may create self-interest. State the known facts, refuse unsupported entries, retain evidence through approved processes and seek an appropriate independent internal escalation route. Do not share confidential documents indiscriminately or announce that fraud is proven when it is only suspected.

#### Practice (20 minutes; 10 marks)

At fictional Cedar Learning, one clerk can create suppliers, enter invoices and release payments. The manager tells the clerk to pay a CU4,800 invoice from an unfamiliar supplier before goods arrive, promising a personal bonus and threatening a poor review if the clerk delays. Purchasing records contain no approved order. A second employee reviews the bank reconciliation monthly.

1. Identify two weaknesses and explain a risk for each (4 marks).
2. Propose one preventive control and one detective control, naming who performs each (2 marks).
3. Identify two ethical threats and explain a suitable immediate response (3 marks).
4. Does monthly bank reconciliation alone prove supplier legitimacy? Explain (1 mark).

#### Worked answer (10 minutes)

- One clerk controls supplier creation and payment release: an improper supplier/payment could be created without independent challenge. A missing authorised purchase order and absent receipt evidence mean the invoice is not substantiated under the ordinary goods-purchase process.
- Preventive: an independent authorised approver checks the supplier, approved order and goods-receipt evidence before release; legitimate advance payments require a separately documented exception approval and contractual support, not a fabricated receipt.
- Detective: an independent reviewer examines new-supplier/change logs and exception-payment reports, documenting follow-up. Monthly bank reconciliation is useful for comparing recorded cash with bank movements but cannot by itself establish the commercial validity of an invoice.
- Bonus pressure creates a self-interest threat; the threatened poor review creates an intimidation threat. The clerk should not release an unsupported payment, should document the factual concern and seek the next independent manager/compliance route under policy. Do not accuse the supplier or manager of proven fraud on these facts alone.

**Exit check (5 minutes):** Is moving all three duties to a shared login a solution? No: shared credentials undermine accountability and do not separate responsibility.

**Primary references:** [ACCA BT/FBT current syllabus landing page, September 2026–June 2027](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/fundamentals-exams-study-resources/f1/syllabus-study-guide.html), verified 19 November 2026. [ACCA BT/FBT 2025–2026 specification](https://www.accaglobal.com/content/dam/acca/global/PDF-students/acca/f1/studyguides/btfbt_s25_a26_syllabus_study_guide.pdf) verifies the internal-control topic historically. ### UP-ACCA-FA-02: Correct errors without abusing a suspense account

**Paper:** Financial Accounting (FA/FFA), September 2026–June 2027. **Duration:** 55 minutes. **Builds on:** UP-ACCA-FA-01 bank reconciliation and basic double entry.

**Outcomes:** distinguish errors affecting trial-balance agreement from balanced errors; calculate corrective journals; clear a suspense balance with an audit trail; determine the effect on profit.

#### Learn (15 minutes)

A suspense account temporarily holds an unexplained imbalance while investigation proceeds. It is not an expense category and should not be cleared by an arbitrary entry. Correct the difference between what was actually posted and what should have been posted.

A complete omission or an entry to the wrong expense account can leave equal debit and credit totals. One-sided posting can create an imbalance. For every correction, preserve the source document and link the approved adjustment to the original posting. A zero suspense balance is useful but does not prove that all records are accurate.

#### Model (5 minutes)

A CU90 cash expense has been credited to bank correctly but its debit was omitted. Credits exceed debits by 90, so the balancing suspense entry is a debit of 90. Correct with Dr Expense 90; Cr Suspense 90. Do not credit bank again: its original posting was correct.

#### Practice (20 minutes; 10 marks)

A fictional studio's trial balance initially has credits exceeding debits by CU420. A debit suspense balance of CU420 is inserted. Investigation identifies exactly these errors:

A. A CU420 rent payment was credited correctly to bank, but its rent debit was omitted.
B. CU900 equipment, available for use on the final day of the period, was debited to repairs expense and credited correctly to bank. For this exercise only, ignore depreciation on that purchase in this period.
C. A CU250 credit sale for completed services was omitted entirely from both sales and receivables.

Prepare all correction journals (6 marks), show suspense clearance (1 mark), calculate the net profit correction (2 marks), and explain which errors did not affect agreement (1 mark).

#### Worked answer (10 minutes)

A: Dr Rent expense 420; Cr Suspense 420. Bank is already correct. Suspense opening debit 420 less corrective credit 420 = **zero**.

B: Dr Equipment 900; Cr Repairs expense 900. This reclassifies the asset without posting bank again. Under the stated simplification, profit increases 900.

C: Dr Receivables 250; Cr Service revenue 250. Both sides were missing, so suspense is not involved. Profit increases 250.

Net profit change = −420 + 900 + 250 = **CU730 increase**. For example, a provisional profit of CU3,000 becomes CU3,730. B and C each preserved debit/credit equality despite being wrong; A alone explains the initial imbalance. Keep all three corrections documented even though only A clears suspense.

**Exit check (5 minutes):** May the bookkeeper credit suspense with 420 and debit miscellaneous expense without investigation? No: identify the missing rent entry and evidence first. A balanced trial balance cannot detect every error.

**Primary reference:** [ACCA FA/FFA syllabus and study guide, September 2026–June 2027](https://www.accaglobal.com/content/dam/acca/global/PDF-students/fia/studyguides/fa_ffa_s26_j27_syllabus_and_study_guide.pdf), opened and verified 19 November 2026. It explicitly includes trial balances, correction of errors and suspense accounts. The exercise figures and worked solution are original.

### Business Management | Level 2 | Checking stock before promising a collection

**Builds on:** planning a reliable workday.

#### Objectives
Distinguish physical stock, reserved stock and available stock; calculate what can be promised; communicate a shortfall accurately.

#### Explanation and worked example
Stock on a shelf is not necessarily available for a new order. Some may already be reserved, damaged or awaiting a check. Northbridge Community Shop physically holds 36 workshop packs. Eight are reserved for existing customers and three are damaged and unavailable. Available stock is 36 − 8 − 3 = 25 packs. A new request for 28 packs has a shortfall of three. An expected delivery is not stock in hand, so staff must not promise it as confirmed availability without verification.

#### Activity
A physical count finds only 34 packs, including the three damaged packs. Reservations remain eight. The customer needs 28 tomorrow. A supplier says five more packs “may arrive tomorrow”. Calculate available stock and the shortfall. Draft a two-sentence response and list one internal action.

#### Indicative answer
Available stock is 34 − 8 − 3 = 23; the shortfall is five. “We currently have 23 packs available for your order. Five more may arrive tomorrow, but I cannot confirm the full 28 until delivery is verified; would you like to discuss a partial collection?” Internally, report and reconcile the two-pack stock discrepancy through the approved stock-control process without inventing a cause. Do not release existing reservations to solve the problem.

#### Facilitation and assessment
Give learners separate physical, reserved and damaged stock cards. Check that they do not subtract damaged stock twice or count an uncertain delivery as available. Accept alternative customer wording if it is clear, honest and does not promise an unavailable service.

### Business Management | Level 4 | Comparing suppliers beyond headline price

**Builds on:** process bottlenecks and service-quality measurement.

#### Objectives
Calculate expected usable output; compare unit cost on a consistent basis; distinguish an estimate from a contractual guarantee.

#### Explanation and worked example
The cheapest quoted unit may not be the cheapest usable unit. A fictional learning centre needs 190 usable printed packs for an event. Supplier A quotes £400 for 200 packs, delivery included, with an estimated 4% unusable rate. Expected usable output is 200 × 0.96 = 192 packs; expected cost per usable pack is £400 ÷ 192 ≈ £2.08. Supplier B quotes £430 for 200 packs with an estimated 1% unusable rate: 198 usable packs and approximately £2.17 each. Neither average guarantees at least 190 usable packs in this particular delivery.

#### Activity
A now charges £30 delivery; B still includes delivery. Recalculate the expected usable-unit costs. Recommend a provisional supplier and identify three checks before commitment. Explain why a reserve of two expected packs is not a reliable contingency plan.

#### Indicative answer
A costs £430 ÷ 192 ≈ £2.24 per expected usable pack. B costs £430 ÷ 198 ≈ £2.17. B is provisionally preferable on these assumptions: equal total cost, more expected usable packs and lower expected usable-unit cost. Verify delivery timing, how defect estimates were established, and replacement/refund arrangements. Also inspect a sample where feasible. A's expected two-pack margin above 190 is only an average-based estimate; actual defects may be higher. Define an appropriate contingency based on credible variability evidence and event needs, not an invented guarantee. Real purchasing still requires the organisation's approval process.

#### Facilitation and assessment
Ask pairs to identify hidden assumptions before calculating. Award interpretation marks for discussing uncertainty and quality, not just selecting B. Extension: ask what evidence would justify paying more for a guaranteed replacement deadline, without inventing contract terms.

### Health and Social Care | Level 3 | Closing the loop on a practical concern

**Builds on:** accurate nonclinical records and handovers.

#### Objectives
Record a concern in the person's own terms; distinguish acknowledging a concern from resolving it; agree a realistic follow-up and verify the outcome.

#### Explanation and worked example
A response is not complete merely because a message was passed on. A closed-loop process identifies an owner, agrees a follow-up, records the action and checks whether the person's concern was addressed. It must not promise a result outside the worker's authority.

At a fictional adult community activity centre, Jo says the weekly timetable uses print that is difficult to read. The worker asks what format Jo would prefer. Jo requests a larger-print paper copy. The worker agrees to ask the activity coordinator and provide an update by 15:00. At 14:30 the coordinator supplies a larger-print copy. The worker checks with Jo whether that copy is usable, rather than assuming the larger font solved the problem. No diagnosis is inferred or required.

#### Activity
At 15:00 the coordinator has not responded. Write an update to Jo and a factual internal note. State the next step if Jo says the proposed larger-print copy still does not help.

#### Indicative answer
Update: “I have not yet received the copy I requested. I will follow up with the coordinator now and agree the next update with you; would you like me to explain today's activities while we wait?” Only offer this if feasible and wanted. Note: “Jo requested a larger-print paper timetable today. Request sent to the activity coordinator; no response received by the agreed 15:00 update. Jo informed; follow-up pending.” Record the actual agreed next update, not a time invented retrospectively. If the copy remains unusable, ask Jo what would help and seek appropriate supervisor support through the centre's process. Do not close the concern simply because a document was delivered.

#### Facilitation and assessment
Use a paired conversation and observer checklist for listening, truthful updates and ownership. No personal disclosure is needed. Assess whether the record separates requested action, completed action and unresolved outcome. Keep discussion nonclinical and use only fictional information.

### Health and Social Care | Level 5 | Evaluating whether feedback represents everyone

**Builds on:** service-access improvement and change governance.

#### Objectives
Calculate response and satisfaction rates; recognise nonresponse bias; propose an inclusive evidence-gathering plan before recommending wider rollout.

#### Explanation and worked example
A high satisfaction percentage may describe respondents rather than everyone using a service. In a fictional adult community centre, 100 people are invited to comment on a new booking arrangement. Twenty reply to an online survey; 18 say they are satisfied. Respondent satisfaction is 18 ÷ 20 = 90%, while the response rate is 20 ÷ 100 = 20%. It is incorrect to conclude that 90% of all 100 invitees are satisfied. The views of 80 nonrespondents are unknown, and people who cannot or prefer not to respond online may be underrepresented.

#### Activity
A manager proposes immediate full rollout, citing “90% approval”. Write a short review recommendation. Include two alternative feedback methods, a safeguard against pressured responses, two additional outcome measures and a condition for postponing the decision.

#### Indicative answer
“Do not treat 90% respondent satisfaction as 90% approval across the service. Keep the rollout decision provisional while collecting more representative evidence.” Offer voluntary paper feedback and private telephone or in-person feedback, with suitable communication support through approved arrangements. Explain that declining or criticising will not affect access to activities; do not have the person promoting the project supervise every response. Also review booking-error rates and waiting times, including the experience of people using non-digital channels. Use only relevant, appropriately authorised data. Postpone wider rollout if material access concerns remain unresolved or important user groups' experiences are still missing. More responses alone do not remove bias, and no response must not be coded as satisfaction.

#### Facilitation and assessment
Have groups rewrite the manager's headline accurately, then challenge each other's evidence plans. Assess denominators, uncertainty and a genuine route for negative feedback. Learners must not contact real service users or collect identifiable data for this exercise.

### CS08-02 — Spreadsheet formulas and checking totals
**Subject:** technology/computer science. **Proposed grade:** 8. **Prerequisite:** multiplication and table coordinates. **Resources:** paper grid or offline spreadsheet. **Objectives:** use cell references; copy a relative formula; verify an output independently. **Starter:** if a spreadsheet displays a number neatly, must it be correct?

**Teach and worked example:** A fictional club buys notebooks and pens. Put headings Item, Quantity, Unit price and Line total in A1:D1. Row 2 is Notebook, 4, 80; row 3 is Pen, 6, 25. In D2 enter `=B2*C2`; result 320. Copying it to D3 normally changes the relative references to `=B3*C3`, giving 150. In D4 enter `=SUM(D2:D3)`, giving 470. These are numeric rupee amounts, not a real purchase. Formatting a cell as currency changes its appearance, not the arithmetic. Hard-coding `=4*80` loses the link to future quantity changes.

**Guided work:** predict the new total if notebook quantity becomes 5, then change only B2. Expected notebook cost 400 and grand total 550. On paper, cross out and update the referenced values. Partners explain why the pen cost stays unchanged.

**Independent worksheet:**
1. Add Eraser, quantity 3, unit price 10 as row 4; move the grand total to row 5. Give formulas for D4 and D5.
2. Using the original notebook quantity 4, calculate the three-item grand total.
3. A learner enters `=B2+C2` for notebook cost. What output appears, and why is it wrong?
4. Explain one way to check a spreadsheet total without trusting its formula.

**Teacher key:** 1. `=B4*C4`; `=SUM(D2:D4)`. 2. 500 rupees. 3. 84 rather than 320; addition does not compute quantity × unit price. 4. Independently calculate each line and sum, or use a second mathematically equivalent check. **Marking:** two formula marks, one total mark, two error-explanation marks, one check mark (6).

**Exit:** when quantity changes, which cells should recalculate and why? Expected affected line and grand total, through references. **Support:** colour the source cells and use repeated addition before multiplication. **Extension/homework:** compare estimated cost 4×80+6×30+3×10=530 with exact 500; explain why an estimate is useful but not the final total. Do not upload real club accounts.

### PH09-02 — Interpreting motion graphs
**Subject:** physics. **Proposed grade:** 9. **Prerequisite:** average speed, axes and units. **Resources:** graph paper and fictional motion record. **Objectives:** interpret distance–time gradient; distinguish a stop from moving backwards; calculate total average speed. **Starter:** does a flat distance–time line mean the object disappears or stops?

**Teach and worked example:** Use a cumulative-distance table: time/s = 0, 2, 4, 6, 8; total distance/m = 0, 4, 8, 8, 14. Plot time horizontally and total distance vertically, connecting points with straight segments for this simplified model. From 0–4 s the gradient is 8/4=2 m/s. From 4–6 s distance is unchanged: speed zero. From 6–8 s speed is (14−8)/(8−6)=3 m/s. Whole-record average speed is 14/8=1.75 m/s, including the stop. The slopes are not the vertical heights of the graph.

**Guided work:** draw the corresponding speed–time graph: 2 m/s for 0–4 s; 0 for 4–6 s; 3 for 6–8 s. Its area is 2×4+0×2+3×2=14 m. Instantaneous jumps in this model simplify real acceleration; do not claim a physical cart changes speed instantly.

**Independent worksheet:**
1. How far has the object travelled at 6 s?
2. Which interval has the greatest speed? Explain with gradient.
3. Calculate average speed for 4–8 s.
4. A learner says a downward segment on a cumulative-distance graph means returning home. Explain the problem.

**Teacher key:** 8 m; 6–8 s, gradient 3 m/s; 6/4=1.5 m/s; cumulative distance cannot decrease. A position/displacement–time graph can slope down to show motion in the negative direction. **Marking:** 1, 2, 2 and 2 marks respectively (7).

**Exit:** distinguish distance–time graph height and gradient: distance travelled versus speed. **Support:** highlight two points and calculate rise/run explicitly. **Extension/homework:** sketch a separate position–time graph for moving 4 m away then returning to the starting position. State that final displacement is zero while total distance is 8 m. No travel near roads or unsupervised physical trial.

### CH09-02 — Electronic structure and simple bonding models
**Subject:** chemistry. **Proposed grade:** 9. **Prerequisite:** atoms, ions, proton/electron counts. **Resources:** paper electron counters. **Objectives:** use a simplified shell model; distinguish electron transfer and sharing; explain a formula ratio without treating it as a molecule count. **Starter:** does forming an ion alter the number of protons?

**Teach and worked example:** For these simple first-20-element examples, neutral sodium has 11 electrons arranged 2,8,1 and chlorine has 17 arranged 2,8,7. Sodium can lose one electron to form Na⁺ (2,8); chlorine can gain one to form Cl⁻ (2,8,8). Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions forms ionic bonding. Solid sodium chloride is a giant ionic lattice, not separate little NaCl molecules. NaCl expresses the simplest 1:1 ion ratio. This shell description is a teaching model, not a universal rule that every atom must gain an octet.

**Guided work:** model magnesium (2,8,2) and chlorine. Magnesium loses two electrons; two chlorine atoms each gain one. Charges Mg²⁺ and two Cl⁻ balance, giving MgCl₂. Do this with paper tokens only; do not handle reactive metals or chlorine.

**Independent worksheet:**
1. Electron count in Na⁺ and Mg²⁺?
2. Why does MgCl₂ require two chloride ions per magnesium ion?
3. In H₂, two hydrogen atoms share a pair of electrons. Is this ionic transfer or covalent sharing?
4. Correct: “An ionic bond is the electron that moves from sodium to chlorine.”

**Teacher key:** 10 in each; +2 balances two −1 charges; covalent sharing; transfer helps create ions, but the ionic bond is electrostatic attraction between opposite charges. **Marking:** two counts 2; charge explanation 2; classification 1; correction 2 (7).

**Exit:** distinguish an ion's charge from the subscript in a formula. Expected charge is net electrical charge; subscript indicates number/ratio. **Support:** separate nucleus counters from electron counters. **Extension/homework:** predict MgO from Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ and explain the 1:1 ratio. Board reviewer must confirm required model depth and notation.

### BI11-02 — Active transport and membrane movement
**Subject:** biology. **Proposed grade:** 11. **Prerequisite:** diffusion, osmosis and concentration gradients. **Resources:** labelled membrane diagram and hypothetical observations. **Objectives:** contrast passive and active movement; link active transport to cellular energy; evaluate evidence without overclaiming. **Starter:** can net movement occur from a lower to a higher concentration?

**Teach and worked example:** Diffusion produces net movement down a concentration gradient. Active transport can move substances against an electrochemical gradient using energy supplied through cellular processes. In this introductory model, a root-hair cell accumulates mineral ions from a more dilute surrounding solution. Membrane proteins provide selective transport. ATP can directly power some pumps; other transport processes depend indirectly on gradients established by energy-requiring pumps. Avoid teaching that every transported molecule directly consumes one ATP.

**Guided work:** hypothetical observations: external ion concentration 1 unit, cell concentration 5 units, with continuing uptake. In a second condition that reduces ATP availability, uptake falls. This supports energy-dependent transport but does not identify a specific protein or rule out all other effects of the treatment. Draw the concentration gradient and the observed uptake arrow separately.

**Independent worksheet:**
1. Which direction is down the stated concentration gradient?
2. Why is continued uptake into the cell not explained by simple diffusion alone?
3. Name a variable to control when comparing uptake rates.
4. Why does reduced uptake after metabolic inhibition not prove exactly which transporter is involved?

**Teacher key:** cell to outside, 5 toward 1; uptake goes opposite the concentration gradient in the simplified scenario; temperature, ion availability, tissue amount or exposure time; inhibition can affect many processes and does not identify a protein. **Marking:** 1+2+1+2=6.

**Exit:** give one passive and one energy-dependent transport example without confusing osmosis with all solute movement. **Support:** use directional arrows and a two-column comparison. **Extension/homework:** explain why a membrane can be selectively permeable without being a solid wall. All observations are fictional; no plant-treatment chemicals or biological specimens are required. More advanced electrical-gradient treatment requires teacher review.

### MA12-02 — Definite integrals and accumulation
**Subject:** mathematics. **Proposed grade:** 12. **Prerequisite:** polynomial derivatives and signed area. **Resources:** graph paper/calculator. **Objectives:** find a polynomial antiderivative; evaluate limits; distinguish displacement from total distance. **Starter:** does an area below the horizontal axis count as positive in a signed integral?

**Teach and worked example:** For positive-integer powers, an antiderivative of xⁿ is xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1), plus a constant. Evaluate a definite integral by F(b)−F(a). If velocity v(t)=2t+1 m/s for 0≤t≤3 s, displacement is ∫₀³(2t+1)dt=[t²+t]₀³=12 m. Because velocity stays positive, distance is also 12 m. Units are (m/s)×s=m. The integration constant cancels when subtracting endpoint values.

**Guided work:** ∫₁³2x dx=[x²]₁³=9−1=8. Check by trapezium area under y=2x: ½(2+6)×2=8. Label that this independent check works because the graph is a straight line.

**Independent worksheet:**
1. Evaluate ∫₀²3x² dx.
2. Evaluate ∫₂⁵4 dx and explain geometrically.
3. For velocity −2 m/s during the first second and +2 m/s during the next second, find displacement and distance.
4. Why is omitting the lower-limit value an error in ∫₁³2x dx?

**Teacher key:** [x³]₀²=8; 12, rectangle height 4 and width 3; displacement 0 m, distance 4 m; the integral accumulates only from 1 to 3, so subtract F(1)=1. **Marking:** 2+2+2+2=8.

**Exit:** state when the integral of velocity equals distance. Expected: for nonnegative velocity over the interval; generally integrate speed for distance. **Support:** mark lower/upper limits in different colours. **Extension/homework:** v=t−1 on [0,2]: displacement 0, distance 1 m by two triangular areas. Exact calculus placement and required notation remain board-dependent.

### EN10-02 — Tone and language choices in nonfiction
**Subject:** English. **Proposed grade:** 10. **Prerequisite:** inference and supporting quotations. **Resources:** original passage below. **Objectives:** explain a language effect in context; distinguish tone from topic; support interpretation without inventing intentions.

**Original passage:** “By Monday, the noticeboard had become a small forest of paper. Notices leaned over notices; last month's timetable still guarded the centre. We did not need another announcement. We needed someone to remove the expired ones, label the remaining sections and leave a little room for tomorrow. A clear board would not solve every problem, but it would give our questions somewhere to begin.”

**Starter:** is the writer simply angry, or is there also humour and a practical purpose? **Teach and worked example:** Naming a technique is not enough. The metaphor “small forest of paper” suggests crowded growth and difficulty finding a path through information. In this context it makes clutter vivid and mildly humorous. The contrast between “another announcement” and organising existing notices shifts attention from quantity to usefulness. Interpretations must explain the words in this passage, not claim that a technique always causes one fixed emotion.

**Guided work:** discuss “last month's timetable still guarded the centre”. Personification makes the outdated timetable seem stubbornly in control. Accept another plausible interpretation if tied to the words and context.

**Independent worksheet:**
1. Identify two signs of the writer's practical purpose.
2. Explain the effect of the short sentence “We did not need another announcement.”
3. Write an 80–100-word analysis of the writer's tone using two brief quotations.
4. Rewrite the opening as a neutral administrative statement, then explain what is lost.

**Teacher key:** removing expired notices, labelling sections, leaving space; the short sentence emphatically rejects adding to clutter and prepares the alternative; tone can be mildly humorous, frustrated yet constructive, supported by evidence; neutral example: “The noticeboard contains overlapping and outdated notices.” It loses vivid imagery and humour. **Rubric:** accurate interpretation 3, precise textual evidence 3, contextual effect explanation 3, clarity 1 (10); technique labels alone do not earn full explanation marks.

**Exit:** distinguish “what is said” from “how it is expressed”. **Support:** frame “The words…suggest…because…”. **Extension/homework:** write a constructive 60-word notice requesting a tidier board, without insulting staff or pupils. Teaching word limits are not claimed examination specifications.

### Lesson 7 — IELTS foundation: comparing options and supporting a choice
**Level/length:** approximately B1–B2, 55 minutes. **Progression:** move from confirming information to comparing evidence and explaining a decision.
**Outcomes:** distinguish fact from preference; compare two options; develop a spoken explanation using a reason, example and limitation.
**Track boundary:** shared language preparation for Academic/General Training. This is not a Life Skills mock. Academic visual-description and General Training letter tasks are different; the optional writing branches below are short preparatory exercises, not full timed test tasks or band assessments.

#### Instructor plan
5 min recall clarification phrases; 10 min vocabulary and notice reading; 10 min listening; 15 min paired comparison; 10 min optional writing; 5 min exit explanation. Teach **whereas**, **although**, **more convenient**, **less expensive** and **on balance**. Ask learners to point to evidence rather than assume that expensive means better.

#### Reading: fictional course options
**Course A:** Mondays and Wednesdays, 18:00–19:00, six weeks, £90 total, online. Recorded explanations are available afterward. Discussion activities happen live.
**Course B:** Saturdays, 10:00–12:00, six weeks, £110 total, at a community centre. Printed worksheets are included. No recordings are offered.
Both courses provide twelve scheduled teaching hours. These are invented options, not UniPathway offers.
Questions: 1. Which costs less? 2. Which includes printed worksheets? 3. Does Course A allow learners to complete live discussion at any time? 4. Which has more scheduled teaching hours? 5. Is transport included in Course B’s price?

#### Listening script
“I first preferred Course B because I enjoy meeting people face to face. However, I work on two Saturdays next month, so I would miss some classes. Course A is cheaper, and I am free on Monday and Wednesday evenings. My internet connection is sometimes unreliable, which is a disadvantage. I will check whether I can use a quiet room with reliable internet before deciding.”
Questions: What was the first preference? Why did it change? What problem remains? Has the speaker definitely booked?

#### Learner practice
1. Complete: “A is cheaper, ___ B includes worksheets.” “___ A has recordings, its discussions are live.”
2. Person X works every Wednesday evening; Person Y cannot travel on Saturdays. Recommend an option for each **only if** its schedule fits, and identify one unresolved question. Do not assume recordings replace all attendance.
3. Give a 60-second comparison: preference, two reasons, one disadvantage, one question to check. Partner asks a clarification question.
4. Optional **Academic preparation:** write 60–80 words comparing the stated prices and scheduled hours objectively. Do not invent a trend or explain causes not supplied.
5. Optional **General Training preparation:** write 60–80 words to a course organiser explaining a schedule problem and asking about missed-class arrangements. This is not a complete examination-length response.

#### Answers and models
Reading: 1 A; 2 B; 3 no, discussions are live; 4 neither, both twelve; 5 not stated. Listening: B; work on two Saturdays; unreliable internet; no.
Gap answers: **whereas / but**; **Although**. X could consider B if Saturdays are free, but must confirm availability; A conflicts with Wednesday work. Y could consider A if both evenings and internet are suitable.
Comparison model: “On balance, I prefer A because it costs £20 less and I can attend in the evening. Both courses offer twelve hours, so the lower price does not mean fewer scheduled hours. However, I need reliable internet. I would ask whether technical problems affect attendance.”
Academic model opening: “Both courses provide twelve scheduled teaching hours over six weeks. Course A costs £90, compared with £110 for Course B.” General Training opening: “I am interested in your evening course, but I work on Wednesdays. Could you explain what happens if a learner misses a live discussion?”
Exit/homework: revise the spoken choice after the instructor changes one availability detail. Feedback: accuracy of facts, clear comparison, justified choice and acknowledgement of uncertainty. Do not assign an IELTS band.

### Lesson 8 — German A1: changing an appointment politely
**Length:** 45 minutes. **Progression:** change the appointment rather than simply accept it.
**Outcomes:** state an existing appointment, explain unavailability briefly, request an alternative and confirm the replacement.
**Language:** “Ich habe am Dienstag einen Termin.” (I have an appointment on Tuesday.) “Ich kann leider nicht kommen.” (Unfortunately I cannot come.) “Kann ich den Termin verschieben?” (Can I move the appointment?) “Geht es am Donnerstag?” (Is Thursday possible?) “Um elf Uhr passt es.” (Eleven o’clock works.) “Also Donnerstag um elf Uhr.” (So, Thursday at eleven.) **Leider** softens an apology; **am** introduces a day, **um** a time. No personal explanation is compulsory.

#### Instructor plan
5 min retrieve days/times; 10 min model request and confirmation; 10 min listen and sequence; 15 min role-play twice; 5 min exit check. Contrast **Dienstag** and **Donnerstag** with written cards before listening.
#### Listening script
A: Guten Tag. Ich habe am Dienstag um zehn Uhr einen Termin. Ich kann leider nicht kommen. Kann ich den Termin verschieben?
B: Ja. Geht es am Mittwoch um neun Uhr?
A: Am Mittwoch arbeite ich. Geht es am Donnerstag?
B: Ja, am Donnerstag um elf Uhr.
A: Gut. Also Donnerstag um elf Uhr. Vielen Dank.
B: Gern. Auf Wiedersehen.
Questions: original day/time? rejected alternative? reason for rejecting it? final day/time?
#### Practice
1. Complete with **habe / kann / verschieben / um**: “Ich ___ einen Termin.” “Ich ___ leider nicht kommen.” “Kann ich den Termin ___?” “Donnerstag ___ elf Uhr.”
2. Put in conversational order: confirm new time; explain existing appointment; request change; consider alternative.
3. Caller has Friday 12:00 but is unavailable; receptionist offers Monday 09:00 or Tuesday 11:00. Caller can attend only Tuesday. Perform the exchange and write the final appointment. Swap roles with new details.
#### Answers/model
Listening: Tuesday 10:00; Wednesday 09:00; work; Thursday 11:00. Gaps: habe, kann, verschieben, um. Order: existing appointment → change request → alternative → confirmation.
Model ending: “Am Montag kann ich nicht. Dienstag um elf Uhr passt es. Danke.” Written note: “Neuer Termin: Dienstag, elf Uhr.”
Exit/homework: caller must politely correct an instructor who repeats Monday instead of Tuesday. Assess final day/time and clarification with the shared rubric. Write a four-line fictional change request for homework.

### Lesson 9 — French A1: finding a classroom and checking directions
**Length:** 45 minutes. **Progression:** use the confirmed timetable to reach the correct room.
**Outcomes:** ask where a room is, understand two simple directions and confirm a floor.
**Language:** “Excusez-moi, où est la salle cinq ?” (Excuse me, where is room five?) “Au premier étage.” (On the first floor above ground.) “Prenez l’escalier.” (Take the stairs.) “Tournez à droite / à gauche.” (Turn right / left.) “C’est au bout du couloir.” (It is at the end of the corridor.) “Il y a un ascenseur ?” (Is there a lift?) “Vous pouvez répéter ?” (Can you repeat?) **Rez-de-chaussée** means ground floor; explicitly label the classroom map to avoid differences in floor-numbering conventions. Do not assume stairs are accessible to everyone.

#### Instructor plan
5 min recall room numbers; 10 min teach directions with arrows; 10 min listening; 15 min map role-play; 5 min exit route. Provide verbal/map alternatives without requiring learners to walk or disclose a disability.
#### Listening script
A: Excusez-moi, où est la salle cinq ?
B: Au premier étage. Prenez l’escalier, puis tournez à droite.
A: Il y a un ascenseur ?
B: Oui, à gauche de l’escalier. Au premier étage, tournez à droite. La salle cinq est au bout du couloir.
A: Au premier étage, à droite, au bout du couloir ?
B: Oui, c’est ça.
Questions: room? floor? where is the lift relative to the stairs? direction after reaching the floor? where along the corridor?
#### Practice
1. Match **à droite / à gauche / au premier étage** to right / first floor above ground / left.
2. Fill in **où / ascenseur / répéter**: “___ est la salle ?” “Il y a un ___ ?” “Vous pouvez ___ ?”
3. Draw a fictional map: stairs and lift at the centre, room 2 on the left and room 5 at the right end of the first-floor corridor. A asks for room 2; B gives directions including a lift option. Swap for room 5.
#### Answers/model
Listening: room 5; first floor above ground; left of the stairs; right; end of corridor. Matching: right; left; first floor above ground. Gaps: Où, ascenseur, répéter.
Model: “Prenez l’ascenseur. Au premier étage, tournez à gauche. La salle deux est à gauche.” Accept clear alternatives consistent with the drawn map.
Exit/homework: learner repeats the route before starting; instructor changes the destination once. Assess correct floor/direction, question and confirmation. Homework: label a fictional map and write three directions.

### Lesson 10 — Italian A1: correcting a ticket detail
**Length:** 45 minutes. **Progression:** identify a discrepancy before accepting an item.
**Outcomes:** compare a requested destination with a printed ticket, explain a simple error politely and confirm the correction.
**Language:** “Mi scusi, c’è un errore.” (Excuse me, there is an error.) “Ho chiesto un biglietto per Milano.” (I asked for a ticket to Milan.) “Qui c’è scritto Roma.” (It says Rome here.) “Può controllare, per favore?” (Can you check, please?) “Ecco il biglietto corretto.” (Here is the correct ticket.) “Adesso va bene.” (Now it is correct.) Teach **ho chiesto** as a useful whole phrase, not a full past-tense lesson. This simulation makes no claim about real refund rights or rail policies.

#### Instructor plan
5 min retrieve destination/ticket vocabulary; 10 min model polite correction; 10 min listening; 15 min pair task; 5 min exit. Use printed fictional tickets and no payment details.
#### Listening script
A: Mi scusi, c’è un errore. Ho chiesto un biglietto per Milano, ma qui c’è scritto Roma.
B: Mi dispiace. Può mostrarmi il biglietto?
A: Certo. Ecco.
B: Sì, ha ragione. Ecco il biglietto corretto: Milano, partenza alle dodici, binario quattro.
A: Milano, alle dodici, binario quattro. Adesso va bene. Grazie.
Questions: requested destination? printed wrong destination? corrected departure time? platform? what does the learner do before accepting?
#### Practice
1. Complete **errore / controllare / corretto**: “C’è un ___.” “Può ___?” “Ecco il biglietto ___.”
2. Choose the polite opening: a “Mi scusi, può controllare?” b “È tutto sbagliato!” Discuss why a is clearer and less confrontational.
3. Customer card: requested Torino at 10:00; ticket says Milano at 10:00. Clerk card: check, acknowledge and issue fictional Torino 10:00, platform 2. Customer must confirm destination, time and platform. Swap with a different destination, not a real booking.
#### Answers/model
Listening: Milano; Roma; 12:00; platform 4; repeats/checks the details. Gaps: errore, controllare, corretto. Polite choice: a.
Model: “Mi scusi, ho chiesto un biglietto per Torino. Qui c’è scritto Milano. Può controllare?” Final confirmation: “Torino, alle dieci, binario due. Grazie.”
Exit/homework: learner finds one error on a fresh fictional ticket and requests a check. Assess accurate correction, politeness, intelligibility and confirmation. Homework: write two request/correction pairs without any real booking reference.

### Lesson 11 — Spanish A1: asking what to bring and checking a change
**Length:** 45 minutes. **Progression:** prepare for a class and react to a revised start time.
**Outcomes:** ask about materials, distinguish old/new times and confirm the final arrangement.
**Language:** “¿Qué necesito llevar?” (What do I need to bring?) “Un cuaderno y un bolígrafo.” (A notebook and a pen.) “¿Necesito un libro?” (Do I need a book?) “No, damos las hojas en clase.” (No, we give out the sheets in class.) “La clase empieza a las siete, no a las seis.” (Class starts at seven, not six.) “Entonces, a las siete.” (So, at seven.) **Necesito + noun** expresses what I need; **no …** can correct information without a long explanation.

#### Instructor plan
5 min retrieve time/room language; 10 min materials vocabulary; 10 min listening; 15 min role-play and short note; 5 min exit. Show objects or accessible descriptions. Do not imply these fictional materials or times are real enrolment instructions.
#### Listening script
A: Buenos días. ¿Qué necesito llevar al curso?
B: Un cuaderno y un bolígrafo.
A: ¿Necesito un libro?
B: No, damos las hojas en clase. Y hay un cambio: el viernes la clase empieza a las siete de la tarde, no a las seis.
A: Entonces, el viernes a las siete. ¿En el aula tres?
B: Sí, en el aula tres.
A: Perfecto. Gracias.
Questions: two items to bring? book required? old time? new time? day and room?
#### Practice
1. Fill in **llevar / libro / siete**: “¿Qué necesito ___?” “¿Necesito un ___?” “La clase empieza a las ___.”
2. Correct this note using the script: “Viernes, 18:00, aula 2. Llevar un libro.”
3. Staff: Monday class changes from 17:00 to 18:00, room 4; bring notebook and pen, no book. Learner: ask materials, check the time and repeat all details. Swap roles with different fictional details.
4. Write a two-sentence confirmation, including what to bring.
#### Answers/model
Listening: notebook and pen; no; 18:00; 19:00; Friday, room 3. Gaps: llevar, libro, siete.
Corrected note: “Viernes, 19:00, aula 3. Llevar un cuaderno y un bolígrafo. No hace falta un libro.”
Role-play confirmation: “Entonces, el lunes a las seis de la tarde, en el aula cuatro. Necesito un cuaderno y un bolígrafo.”
Exit/homework: learner hears one last time correction and updates the note accurately. Score the shared rubric; essential day/time/room must be correct for task completion. Homework: create a fictional class checklist and ask a partner three preparation questions.
