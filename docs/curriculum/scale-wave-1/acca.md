# ACCA preparation: 23 items

**Staff review only.** Original formative practice, not official questions, exam eligibility, formal ethics completion or accredited evidence. Check the applicable [ACCA qualification structure](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/getting-started/acca-qualification-structure.html), [Foundation Diploma](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/qualifications/glance/foundation-level/foundation-diploma.html) and the nominated sitting's paper/variant. ## ACC-01 · Correcting a customer receipt entry

**Audience:** Foundations in Accountancy · FA1

**Objective:** Use a correcting journal without recording the receipt twice.

**Worked example:** A £180 receipt from a credit customer was posted Dr Bank £180, Cr Sales £180. Bank is already right. Reverse the wrong sales credit and credit the customer receivable: Dr Sales £180, Cr Receivables £180.

**Practice:** A £240 customer receipt was posted correctly to Bank but credited to Other Income instead of Receivables. Write the correction.

**Answer / success criteria:** Dr Other Income £240; Cr Receivables £240. Do not adjust Bank again.

## ACC-02 · Checking timesheets before job costing

**Audience:** Foundations in Accountancy · MA1

**Objective:** Reconcile time records and separate chargeable from support hours.

**Worked example:** A workshop log has three entries: job A 2.5 hours, job B 3 hours, training 1.5 hours. At £12 per hour, assign £30 to A and £36 to B, with £18 separately identified as training. Control total: £84 for 7 hours.

**Practice:** At £15 per hour, allocate 1.5 hours to X, 2 hours to Y and 0.5 hours to training. Reconcile the total.

**Answer / success criteria:** X £22.50; Y £30; training £7.50; total £60, matching four hours at £15.

## ACC-03 · From opening balances to closing equity

**Audience:** Foundations in Accountancy · FA2

**Objective:** Separate profit, drawings and additional capital.

**Worked example:** A sole trader starts with £8,200 capital, earns £2,350 profit, withdraws £900 and adds £400 cash as capital. Closing capital is £8,200 + £2,350 − £900 + £400 = £10,050. Drawings do not reduce profit.

**Practice:** Opening capital is £12,000; loss £800; drawings £550; additional capital £2,000. Find closing capital.

**Answer / success criteria:** £12,650. Treat the loss and drawings as reductions, and new capital as an increase.

## ACC-04 · Comparing expenses using equivalent periods

**Audience:** Foundations in Accountancy · MA2

**Objective:** Convert a recurring cost to a comparable period before recommending a supplier.

**Worked example:** One courier costs £240 each four-week period, another £275 per calendar month. Over a 52-week comparison the first costs 13 × £240 = £3,120 and the second costs 12 × £275 = £3,300. Equal service assumed, first saves £180.

**Practice:** Compare £196 per four weeks with £216 per calendar month over 52 weeks; state what to verify besides price.

**Answer / success criteria:** £2,548 vs £2,592; first saves £44. Verify comparable volume, capacity and service terms.

## ACC-05 · Designing a second check for supplier changes

**Audience:** Applied Knowledge · BT / FIA · FBT

**Objective:** Explain why initiation, verification and approval should be separated.

**Worked example:** A supplier email requests new payment details. The clerk logs it without editing the record; a second staff member verifies using an existing trusted contact; an authorised colleague approves and documents the change. An audit trail supports follow-up.

**Practice:** One employee must cover administration at a small store. Design a compensating review and identify the residual risk.

**Answer / success criteria:** The owner checks the verified details and evidence and approves the change before the next payment; still monitor the risk of collusion or unreviewed emergency bypass.

## ACC-06 · Inventory held on consignment

**Audience:** Applied Knowledge · FA / FIA · FFA

**Objective:** Use ownership to identify whether physical stock belongs in the entity's count.

**Worked example:** A warehouse counts stock costing £9,000, including £1,200 held on behalf of a supplier. It also owns £800 stock held by an agent elsewhere. Assuming no other adjustments and costs recoverable, own inventory is £8,600.

**Practice:** Stock on site costs £14,000; third-party consignment stock there costs £2,100; owned stock held elsewhere costs £1,300. Calculate inventory.

**Answer / success criteria:** £13,200 = £14,000 − £2,100 + £1,300. Confirm ownership from agreements rather than location alone.

## ACC-07 · Choosing a limiting-factor product mix

**Audience:** Applied Knowledge · MA / FIA · FMA

**Objective:** Rank products by contribution per hour, not contribution per unit.

**Worked example:** A contributes £18 and takes 3 hours; B contributes £14 and takes 2 hours. Rates are £6/hour and £7/hour. With 10 hours and a demand cap of two B units, make two B then two A for contribution £64.

**Practice:** With 12 hours and a cap of three B units, what mix maximises contribution under the same rates and no cap on A?

**Answer / success criteria:** Three B use six hours; two A use six. Contribution is £78, assuming whole units only and unchanged contribution/constraints.

## ACC-08 · Reading an offer deadline in a fictional rule set

**Audience:** Applied Skills · LW

**Objective:** Distinguish sending an acceptance from receiving one when the rule requires receipt.

**Worked example:** In the fictional exercise, an offer requires acceptance received by 16:00. Accepted email sent 15:57, received 16:01; condition unmet under that stipulated rule. This is not a rule for any real jurisdiction.

**Practice:** An offer states receipt by 12:00; signed file is sent at 11:58 and received at 11:59. What timing condition is satisfied and what uncertainty remains?

**Answer / success criteria:** Receipt meets the deadline. Timing alone does not establish a valid contract; check the other stated terms and applicable syllabus variant.

## ACC-09 · Evaluating a rate and efficiency trade-off

**Audience:** Applied Skills · PM

**Objective:** Separate price and productivity effects using standard hours for the actual output.

**Worked example:** Standard 2 hours per item at £10/hour. Actual 50 items take 90 hours at £12/hour. Rate variance: 90 × (£12 − £10) = £180 adverse. Efficiency: (100 − 90) × £10 = £100 favourable. Net £80 adverse.

**Practice:** Output is 60, with 115 hours at £11/hour and the same standard. Compute both variances and net.

**Answer / success criteria:** Rate £115 adverse; efficiency £50 favourable; net £65 adverse. Reconcile £1,265 actual less £1,200 standard cost for actual output.

## ACC-10 · Building a tax bridge from a fictional policy

**Audience:** Applied Skills · TX

**Objective:** Distinguish adjusted profit, deductions and tax under given hypothetical rules.

**Worked example:** Illustrative policy only: profit £22,000 contains £1,000 non-deductible expense; an extra £3,000 deduction is allowed. Adjusted base £20,000; exercise tax at 10% is £2,000. The policy/rate are invented and not tax guidance.

**Practice:** Using the same fictional policy, profit £34,000 contains £2,400 non-deductible costs and the extra deduction is £6,400. Calculate base and tax.

**Answer / success criteria:** £30,000 base; £3,000 exercise tax. Select the correct jurisdiction and session syllabus before using any live rate or rule.

## ACC-11 · Separating cash receipts from revenue recognition

**Audience:** Applied Skills · FR

**Objective:** Allocate consideration to separate obligations using relative stand-alone prices in a simplified contract.

**Worked example:** A £900 prepaid bundle includes equipment (stand-alone £800) and support (stand-alone £400). Allocate £600 and £300. If equipment is delivered immediately and support is then provided evenly over six months, recognise £600 on delivery and £50/month over the support period.

**Practice:** Repeat for a £1,200 bundle, equipment £1,000 and four-month support £500 stand-alone, with delivery first and support evenly over time.

**Answer / success criteria:** Allocate £800 to equipment and £400 to support. Recognise £800 on qualifying delivery and £100 each support month, assuming no allocation exception.

## ACC-12 · Auditing sales cut-off in both directions

**Audience:** Applied Skills · AA

**Objective:** Link year-end delivery records and invoices to a financial-statement assertion.

**Worked example:** Year ends 31 December, fictional transfer on delivery. An invoice dated 30 December relates to goods delivered 3 January. The team compares invoices and delivery evidence around year end and challenges recognition in December.

**Practice:** An approved delivery on 31 December is invoiced 2 January. What is the contrasting risk and how could you test it?

**Answer / success criteria:** Sales may be omitted from December or posted in the wrong period. Trace year-end delivery documents to invoices and ledger dates; confirm transfer terms.

## ACC-13 · Computing cash tied up in a working-capital cycle

**Audience:** Applied Skills · FM

**Objective:** Calculate cycle days and state a trade-off before proposing a change.

**Worked example:** Inventory stays 28 days, customers take 36 days to pay, and suppliers allow 22 days. Cycle = 28 + 36 − 22 = 42 days; improving collections by six days gives 36 days if other factors stay fixed.

**Practice:** A firm has 40 inventory days, 32 receivable days and 25 payable days. What if supplier payment accelerates by five days?

**Answer / success criteria:** Cycle lengthens from 47 to 52 days. Compare effects on liquidity against any discount or supply benefit.

## ACC-14 · Recommending a reversible service expansion

**Audience:** Strategic Professional Essentials · SBL

**Objective:** Compare pilot choices using a clear decision criterion and monitoring trigger.

**Worked example:** Pilot A serves one district and can be stopped weekly; B requires a six-month contract. Demand evidence is thin and both staff plans are feasible. Recommend A as a reversible test, with a six-week review of margins and punctuality.

**Practice:** Design a stop/go test when the main uncertainty is customer retention rather than first-order demand.

**Answer / success criteria:** Use repeat purchase rate over an agreed window by acquisition cohort, control for season/discount, set a pre-agreed threshold and budget cap, and schedule independent review before expansion.

## ACC-15 · Comparing discount-rate sensitivity to headroom

**Audience:** Strategic Professional Essentials · SBR

**Objective:** Evaluate possible impairment under two recoverable-amount scenarios.

**Worked example:** A cash-generating unit has carrying amount £4.8m and recoverable amount £5.0m. Headroom is £0.2m. A credible alternative estimate of £4.5m implies £0.3m impairment before considering allocation; management must not simply choose the convenient scenario.

**Practice:** At £7.1m carrying amount, compare £7.4m and £6.9m recoverable amounts. Name one input to scrutinise.

**Answer / success criteria:** £0.3m headroom and £0.2m indicative impairment respectively. Check consistency of discount rate and forecast cash flows, their evidence and relevant disclosure.

## ACC-16 · Separating project value from financing effects

**Audience:** Strategic Professional Options · AFM

**Objective:** Use a simple adjusted present value bridge without double-counting.

**Worked example:** A project's all-equity NPV is £230k. Financing tax benefits have present value £55k, financing fees £20k, expected distress cost £40k. Adjusted present value is £225k, assuming effects are independent and not already in base NPV.

**Practice:** Recalculate for base £100k, tax benefit £35k, fees £17k and distress costs £50k; discuss one risk.

**Answer / success criteria:** £68k APV. Model financing side effects consistently for timing/risk and ensure neither fees nor tax benefits are already in the base case.

## ACC-17 · Combining speed with quality measures

**Audience:** Strategic Professional Options · APM

**Objective:** Explain how a target can induce gaming and design a paired measure.

**Worked example:** A dispatch team targets cases closed within 24 hours. Closures rise but reopened cases double. Pair speed with seven-day reopen rate, define cohorts by original case ID, and sample closed cases for completeness.

**Practice:** A call centre cuts average handling time from seven to five minutes but repeat calls rise. Propose a stronger measure set.

**Answer / success criteria:** Combine time with resolution of the same query over a defined follow-up window and quality sampling; compare like-for-like complexity and investigate causes instead of assuming gaming.

## ACC-18 · Comparing policy scenarios with different cash timing

**Audience:** Strategic Professional Options · ATX

**Objective:** Use explicitly fictional tax timing to explain a present-value trade-off, not to give tax advice.

**Worked example:** Fictional scheme A saves £10k now; B saves £12k once in two years. At 10% exercise discount rate, B is £12k/1.1² ≈ £9.92k today. A is narrowly larger in present-value terms if all other effects match.

**Practice:** At the same discount rate compare an £8k saving now with a £10k saving in two years, and identify one omitted condition.

**Answer / success criteria:** £8k vs about £8.26k, so delayed is larger under assumptions. Check eligibility uncertainty and any other cash, disclosure or compliance effects under the applicable variant.

## ACC-19 · Evaluating contradictory audit evidence

**Audience:** Strategic Professional Options · AAA

**Objective:** Choose follow-up work that responds to a material evidence conflict.

**Worked example:** Management claims a major debtor will pay, but a subsequent bank extract shows only a small receipt and legal correspondence shows a dispute. Investigate the balance and timing, obtain independent corroboration where appropriate, evaluate the estimate and document implications.

**Practice:** Management gives a written representation that a claim is settled; an external letter describes unresolved negotiation. How should the team respond?

**Answer / success criteria:** Reconcile dates and case scope, seek further independent evidence and assess recognition/disclosure implications with engagement leadership. Management representation alone does not resolve conflicting evidence.

## ACC-20 · Responding to pressure to hide a material error

**Audience:** Professional ethics · Foundations in Professionalism / EPSM support

**Objective:** Apply integrity and objectivity to a fictional scenario and choose a proportionate escalation path.

**Worked example:** A manager asks an analyst to conceal a reporting error. The analyst records facts securely, explains why correction is needed, seeks independent guidance under policy, escalates through appropriate channels if unresolved, and does not knowingly sign a misleading report.

**Practice:** An approver urges you to delay a known correction until after a board presentation. Draft a two-sentence response and name one next step if it is refused.

**Answer / success criteria:** Example: “The deck would materially misstate the position without this adjustment. I can prepare a corrected slide and note the impact before the board meets.” If unresolved, use the authorised escalation path and preserve evidence confidentially. This lesson is not official module completion.

## ACC-21 · Choose evidence for occurrence rather than completeness

**Audience:** Current CAT specialist option · FAU

**Objective:** Distinguish the direction of a basic sales evidence test.

**Worked example:** To test whether recorded sales occurred, start from a ledger sale and seek dispatch evidence from another record; to test that dispatches are included, start from dispatch notes and trace into the ledger.

**Practice:** Entries S51 and S52 are in a fictional sales ledger. No matching dispatch record is readily available for S52. Which assertion is initially in question and what should the reviewer do next?

**Answer / success criteria:** Occurrence is initially in question. Seek alternative evidence and investigate the missing link; absence of one record is not conclusive proof of a fictitious sale.

## ACC-22 · Keep gross, net and tax in separate columns

**Audience:** Current CAT specialist option · FTX introductory skills

**Objective:** Reconcile the components of a fictional tax-inclusive invoice without assuming any real rate.

**Worked example:** A made-up levy of 20% of a net 75 credits gives 15 credits levy and 90 gross. To recover net from the gross, divide by 1.20.

**Practice:** An imaginary system applies a 25% levy to net. Split a gross total of 150 credits into net and levy and cross-check.

**Answer / success criteria:** 150 ÷ 1.25 = 120 net; 30 levy. Check 25% of 120 = 30 and 120 + 30 = 150. This is arithmetic practice only, not a jurisdiction rule.

## ACC-23 · Find a cash shortfall before it happens

**Audience:** Current CAT specialist option · FFM

**Objective:** Calculate closing cash and compare it with a minimum buffer.

**Worked example:** Opening cash is 600 credits. Receipts of 420 and payments of 840 leave 180. Against a minimum of 250, the shortfall is 70.

**Practice:** Opening cash is 300, confirmed receipts 440 and scheduled payments 620. A further 90 due from a customer may be late. Compare closing cash in both cases with a buffer of 200.

**Answer / success criteria:** Without the 90: 120, shortfall 80. With it: 210, surplus 10. Use the first case for a contingency rather than treating the possible receipt as certain.
