# Pakistan and Cambridge school tuition: 23 items

**Staff review only.** Original learning activities for staff review. Pakistan grade labels are suggested audiences, not confirmation of board/session alignment. For Cambridge, consult the partial mappings and review rules in the [coverage register](coverage-register.md). Science examples use supplied paper or simulated data rather than practical procedures.

## SCH-01 · Ratios in a print budget

**Audience:** Grade 8

**Objective:** Find a missing part and scale a ratio without changing the proportion.

**Worked example:** A hall prints 75 passes in a 2:3 morning-to-afternoon ratio. Five parts make 75, so each part is 15; the counts are 30 and 45.

**Practice:** Split 105 passes in a 3:4 ratio. Then find the counts if 14 more passes are available at the same ratio.

**Answer / success criteria:** 45 and 60; with 119 passes the counts are 51 and 68.

## SCH-02 · Two cups and a fair cooling test

**Audience:** Grade 8 General Science

**Objective:** Choose a control and separate evidence from an overgeneralised conclusion.

**Worked example:** Two equal cups of water start at 50°C. The wrapped and unwrapped cups read 44°C and 39°C after ten minutes. The observed losses are 6°C and 11°C, so wrapping reduced the measured loss in this trial.

**Practice:** One pair starts at 45°C and 60°C, then reads 40°C and 49°C. Explain one design flaw and suggest two other variables to hold constant.

**Answer / success criteria:** Starting temperatures differ, so the comparison is unfair; hold volume and cup material fixed, and accept equal exposure time or surroundings.

## SCH-03 · A paragraph that defines a school rule

**Audience:** Grade 8

**Objective:** Use a definition, a reason and an example to make guidance unambiguous.

**Worked example:** “A quiet study zone is a space where conversation stays below normal speaking volume. This helps readers concentrate. Take group discussions to the corridor seating area.” Each sentence serves a different purpose.

**Practice:** Write three sentences defining a library's device-charging rule, why it exists and how to follow it.

**Answer / success criteria:** One acceptable response: “The charging station is the only place where pupils may plug in devices. This keeps cables away from walkways. Put a tablet on the station shelf before plugging it in.”

## SCH-04 · مرکزی خیال اور معاون تفصیل

**Audience:** Grade 8 Urdu enrichment

**Objective:** مختصر اردو پیراگراف کا مرکزی خیال اور ایک معاون تفصیل الگ کریں۔

**Worked example:** “اسکول نے پانی بچانے کی مہم شروع کی۔ طلبہ نے ٹپکتے نلکوں کی اطلاع دی اور پانی کے استعمال کا ریکارڈ رکھا۔” مرکزی خیال مہم ہے؛ ٹپکتے نلکوں کی اطلاع دینا معاون تفصیل ہے۔

**Practice:** “محلے میں مطالعے کا حلقہ قائم ہوا۔ بچے جمعے کو کتابیں بدلتے ہیں اور اگلے ہفتے ان پر گفتگو کرتے ہیں۔” مرکزی خیال اور ایک معاون تفصیل لکھیں۔

**Answer / success criteria:** مرکزی خیال: محلے میں مطالعے کا حلقہ قائم ہوا۔ تفصیل: بچے جمعے کو کتابیں بدلتے ہیں یا اگلے ہفتے ان پر گفتگو کرتے ہیں۔

## SCH-05 · Reading a map scale

**Audience:** Grade 9

**Objective:** Convert between map and ground distances and distinguish route from straight-line distance.

**Worked example:** At 1:50,000, 1 cm represents 0.5 km. A 6 cm route measures about 3 km, even if the endpoints are closer in a straight line.

**Practice:** A footpath is 7.4 cm on a 1:25,000 map. Endpoints are 5 cm apart. Calculate both distances and explain the difference.

**Answer / success criteria:** Route 1.85 km; straight line 1.25 km. The route follows bends so it is longer.

## SCH-06 · Power in a low-voltage device

**Audience:** Grade 9 Physics

**Objective:** Distinguish energy, power and time and calculate power from current and voltage.

**Worked example:** A fictional 5 V device drawing 0.40 A uses 2.0 W. In 30 seconds it transfers 60 J at constant power; watts measure rate, not total energy.

**Practice:** Device A draws 0.25 A at 8 V for 12 s; B draws 0.50 A at 4 V for 20 s. Compare power and energy.

**Answer / success criteria:** Both use 2 W. A transfers 24 J; B transfers 40 J because it operates longer.

## SCH-07 · Counting atoms after balancing

**Audience:** Grade 9 Chemistry

**Objective:** Balance a symbolic equation by changing coefficients only and show the conservation check.

**Worked example:** Balance N₂ + H₂ → NH₃. Place 2 before NH₃ to balance nitrogen and 3 before H₂ to balance hydrogen. Both sides then contain 2 N and 6 H atoms.

**Practice:** Balance Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃ and list the atom counts for the final equation. This is a paper exercise, not an experiment.

**Answer / success criteria:** 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃; each side has 4 Fe and 6 O atoms.

## SCH-08 · Resolve rule precedence

**Audience:** Grade 9 Computing

**Objective:** Explain how conditions are ordered when two can be true at once.

**Worked example:** For a fictional attendance log, mark CANCELLED if a class is cancelled; otherwise, mark FULL when enrolled is at least capacity; otherwise, mark OPEN. A cancelled class with 20 enrolled and capacity 20 is CANCELLED, not FULL.

**Practice:** Write a three-branch if/else rule for a prop library: repair required means HOLD; otherwise a reserved prop means RESERVED; all other props show AVAILABLE. Predict outputs for flags repair/reserved equal to true/true, false/true, and false/false.

**Answer / success criteria:** Check repair first; else check reserved; else output AVAILABLE. The outputs in order are HOLD, RESERVED, AVAILABLE.

## SCH-09 · Changing the subject when it appears twice

**Audience:** Grade 10 Maths

**Objective:** Factor a repeated variable to isolate it; state the excluded denominator value.

**Worked example:** From p = ax + bx + c, subtract c and factor to get p−c = x(a+b), so x = (p−c)/(a+b) if a+b ≠ 0. A zero sum instead leaves p=c as a consistency condition.

**Practice:** Rearrange 2m + qm = r−4 for m. Explain what happens when q = −2 for r = 4 and r = 10.

**Answer / success criteria:** m = (r−4)/(q+2) for q ≠ −2; if q=−2, r=4 permits any m, but r=10 gives no solution.

## SCH-10 · Surface area and volume in cell models

**Audience:** Grade 10 Biology

**Objective:** Calculate surface-area-to-volume ratios and qualify what a geometric model predicts.

**Worked example:** A cube of side 2 units has area 24 square units and volume 8 cubic units, a ratio of 3 per unit. For side 1, the ratio is 6 per unit; smaller blocks have more exposed area per unit volume.

**Practice:** Compare one cube of side 4 cm with eight separate cubes of side 2 cm at equal total volume. Explain whether this alone gives a cell's diffusion time.

**Answer / success criteria:** Both total 64 cm³. Areas are 96 cm² and 192 cm²; ratios are 1.5 and 3 cm⁻¹. No: diffusion also depends on concentration gradients, distance and membrane properties.

## SCH-11 · Reading an implied attitude

**Audience:** Grade 10 English

**Objective:** Infer attitude from precise wording and separate an inference from a stated fact.

**Worked example:** “At last, the caretaker handed over the key.” The handing over is stated; “at last” suggests the speaker found the wait long, but does not prove the caretaker caused it.

**Practice:** Find one fact and one supported inference: “Naturally, the poster appeared the day after the competition closed.” Offer an alternative reading.

**Answer / success criteria:** The poster appeared after closure; “naturally” may be ironic, implying predictable disorganisation. It can also be a literal comment about an expected schedule if surrounding text supports that.

## SCH-12 · Comparing development indicators

**Audience:** Grade 10 Pakistan Studies enrichment

**Objective:** Distinguish totals, rates and access measures when comparing fictional districts.

**Worked example:** District A's 80 staffed schools serve 40,000 pupils (2 per 1,000); B's 60 serve 20,000 (3 per 1,000). More schools does not imply greater per-pupil availability, and neither rate measures travel time.

**Practice:** District C has 18 clinics for 90,000 people; D has 15 for 50,000. Compute clinics per 10,000 residents and name a missing access measure.

**Answer / success criteria:** C has 2 and D has 3 clinics per 10,000. Travel time, opening hours or staffing would add information about actual access.

## SCH-13 · Using a discriminant to classify roots

**Audience:** Grade 11 Maths

**Objective:** Use b²−4ac to predict real intersections before solving a quadratic.

**Worked example:** For x²−6x+9, D=36−36=0, so the graph touches the x-axis at one repeated real root x=3. A negative D means no real roots.

**Practice:** Classify x²−4x+5. For x²−4x+k, find k for a repeated root and the interval allowing two distinct real roots.

**Answer / success criteria:** D=−4 gives no real roots. D=16−4k, so k=4 gives a repeat and k<4 gives two distinct real roots.

## SCH-14 · Momentum from an impulse graph

**Audience:** Grade 11 Physics

**Objective:** Calculate net impulse as signed area and use it to update momentum.

**Worked example:** A constant +8 N force for 0.25 s gives +2 N·s. If initial momentum is −1 kg·m/s, final momentum is +1 kg·m/s because impulse changes momentum, not initial position.

**Practice:** A net force rises linearly from 0 to 12 N over 0.40 s, then stays at −4 N for 0.10 s. Find net impulse and final momentum from +1.5 kg·m/s.

**Answer / success criteria:** Triangle gives +2.4 N·s, rectangle −0.4 N·s; net +2.0 N·s and final +3.5 kg·m/s.

## SCH-15 · Limiting reactants on paper

**Audience:** Grade 11 Chemistry

**Objective:** Compare amounts in moles using stoichiometric coefficients and calculate a leftover amount.

**Worked example:** For hypothetical bookkeeping A + 2B → C, 3.0 mol A and 4.0 mol B allow at most 2.0 mol C. B is limiting; 1.0 mol A remains when the assumed reaction goes to completion.

**Practice:** For 2X + 3Y → 4Z, start with 5.0 mol X and 6.0 mol Y. Find the limiting reactant, theoretical Z and leftover X.

**Answer / success criteria:** Y limits the reaction extent to 2.0 mol; 8.0 mol Z forms, 4.0 mol X is used and 1.0 mol X remains.

## SCH-16 · Explain when an index becomes stale

**Audience:** Grade 11 Computing

**Objective:** Distinguish records from the index that locates them and update the index after a change.

**Worked example:** A paper index maps F12 to row 3 and F17 to row 4. Inserting a new data record before row 3 moves F12 to row 4 and F17 to row 5. If the index is not updated, an exact ID lookup can retrieve the wrong row.

**Practice:** An index maps D2 to row 4, D7 to row 6 and D9 to row 7. Delete row 5, then write the corrected index. State one check before returning a found record.

**Answer / success criteria:** D2 remains row 4, D7 moves to 5 and D9 moves to 6. Verify the returned record ID still matches the lookup ID.

## SCH-17 · Expected values and break-even decisions

**Audience:** Grade 12 Maths

**Objective:** Compute long-run expected net return and distinguish it from a guaranteed result.

**Worked example:** A battery test costs Rs 40 and returns a Rs 150 saving with probability 0.4, otherwise zero. Expected net saving is 0.4×150−40=Rs 20; individual trials can still lose Rs 40.

**Practice:** A quality check costs Rs 60 and saves Rs 240 only when a flaw is found. Find break-even probability and expected value at p=0.30.

**Answer / success criteria:** Break-even p=0.25; expected net value at 0.30 is Rs 12. This is a model, not a prediction for one unit.

## SCH-18 · Reading a capacitor discharge curve

**Audience:** Grade 12 Physics

**Objective:** Use a fraction of initial voltage to identify a time constant in theoretical data.

**Worked example:** For V=V₀ exp(−t/RC), the voltage at t=RC is approximately 0.368V₀. From 10.0 V falling to 3.68 V after 2.0 s, the time constant is 2.0 s.

**Practice:** In a simulation, V₀=8.0 V and V=2.94 V at 0.50 s. Estimate RC and voltage at 1.00 s.

**Answer / success criteria:** RC≈0.50 s; at two time constants V≈8e⁻²≈1.08 V.

## SCH-19 · Comparing allele and phenotype proportions

**Audience:** Grade 12 Biology

**Objective:** Use an idealised one-locus random-mating model to estimate genotype proportions and name a model assumption.

**Worked example:** With recessive allele frequency q=0.20 and p=0.80, expected genotype frequencies are p²=0.64, 2pq=0.32 and q²=0.04 under Hardy–Weinberg conditions. Recessive phenotype is not equal to recessive allele frequency.

**Practice:** In a fictional population, aa frequency is 0.09 under the model. Calculate q, p and the heterozygote proportion. Give one reason the model may not match an actual population.

**Answer / success criteria:** q=0.30, p=0.70, and 2pq=0.42. Non-random mating, selection, migration or genetic drift may affect observed proportions.

## SCH-20 · Testing the scope of a persuasive claim

**Audience:** Grade 12 English

**Objective:** Revise a generalisation to match the size and source of its evidence.

**Worked example:** “Online revision always raises grades” is too broad if evidence is a four-week trial of one class. “Scores rose in the class using online revision during the trial” matches the evidence; without a comparison group causation is not established.

**Practice:** A survey of 30 volunteers from one city school found 21 preferred printed notes. Write a faithful claim and explain one limit.

**Answer / success criteria:** “Twenty-one of 30 volunteers at one city school preferred printed notes.” A self-selected small local sample cannot justify a nationwide claim.

## SCH-21 · Compare a change fairly

**Audience:** Cambridge Lower Secondary · numeracy

**Objective:** Compare the same absolute increase against two different starting values.

**Worked example:** Two clubs each gain six members. A goes from 20 to 26, a 30% rise; B goes from 30 to 36, a 20% rise. The denominator is the starting group.

**Practice:** A goes from 16 to 20 and B from 24 to 30. Identify the bigger absolute rise and compare the relative rises.

**Answer / success criteria:** B adds six versus four, but both rise by 25%.

## SCH-22 · Repeated interest on a fictional balance

**Audience:** Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 · C1.13(4) and E1.13(4) practice candidate

**Objective:** Distinguish a fixed annual increment from a multiplier applied to a changing balance.

**Worked example:** An imaginary 200 credits at 5% for two years becomes 220 with simple interest; compounding gives 200 × 1.05² = 220.50 because the second year also earns on the first year's interest.

**Practice:** A balance of 400 credits grows by 10% yearly for three years. Compare simple and annually compounded outcomes and explain their difference.

**Answer / success criteria:** Simple: 520; compound: 532.40; compound is 12.40 higher because later interest includes earlier interest.

## SCH-23 · Spot the offset in a measurement series

**Audience:** Cambridge International AS Physics 9702 · 1.3(1)–(2) practice candidate

**Objective:** Distinguish systematic zero error from random scatter and precision from accuracy.

**Worked example:** A simulated instrument should read 0.00 cm when closed, but reads 0.20 cm. Repeats of 5.20, 5.21 and 5.19 cm cluster tightly, yet their mean is 0.20 cm high from the offset. Repetition reduces the effect of random scatter on the mean, not the offset.

**Practice:** A sensor reads −0.10 cm at zero. A sample reads 7.36, 7.40 and 7.38 cm. Give the mean after zero correction and say which observation shows precision.

**Answer / success criteria:** Mean observed is 7.38 cm; subtract −0.10 cm to obtain 7.48 cm. The small 0.04 cm range shows tight agreement. This does not rule out other bias.
