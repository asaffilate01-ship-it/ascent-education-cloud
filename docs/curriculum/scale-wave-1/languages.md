# IELTS and other languages: 24 items

**Staff review only.** These are original skill drills, not full-length tests or immigration advice. See the official [IELTS Academic](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test) and [General Training](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-general-training-test) formats before assembling a mock. Use a tutor or peer to read Listening prompts aloud and practise Speaking. The everyday language scenarios are general communication practice, not proof of visa eligibility.

## LAN-01 · Overview before details

**Audience:** IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

**Objective:** Summarise the overall pattern in one sentence without guessing why it occurred.

**Worked example:** Fictional museum visits in June and July: West 120→160, East 80→70. Model: Overall, West remained the busier museum and gained visitors, whereas East recorded a small decline.

**Practice:** Write one overview from these fictional library visits: North 200→220, South 90→150, May to June. Then give one comparison using the increases.

**Answer / success criteria:** Overall, both libraries attracted more visitors, and North remained busier. South recorded the larger increase, gaining 60 visits compared with 20 at North.

## LAN-02 · An enquiry letter with three clear points

**Audience:** IELTS General Training Writing Task 1

**Objective:** Use a polite opening followed by three separate practical requests.

**Worked example:** A model excerpt: Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to ask about your weekend cooking class. Could you tell me whether beginners can enrol, what equipment I should bring and how much the course costs?

**Practice:** Draft an opening and three questions to a community centre about start time, accessibility and whether a partner may join a photography workshop.

**Answer / success criteria:** Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to enquire about your photography workshop. Could you tell me when it starts, whether the room has step-free access and whether my partner can attend with me?

## LAN-03 · Develop a claim with evidence

**Audience:** IELTS Academic and General Training Writing Task 2

**Objective:** Write a mini-paragraph that links a claim, specific example and limitation.

**Worked example:** Claim: Evening library hours help some working adults. Example: A commuter finishing at six could collect books at seven. Limitation: A branch with little evening demand might make better use of one late day per week.

**Practice:** Write three linked sentences about keeping one public sports centre open on Sunday. Include one trade-off.

**Answer / success criteria:** Sunday opening would give people with Saturday shifts more chances to exercise. For example, a retail worker could join a Sunday swimming session. However, a short pilot is wiser than full-day opening if staff costs would be high.

## LAN-04 · Spot an unsupported inference

**Audience:** IELTS Academic Reading

**Objective:** Distinguish a direct contradiction from a claim not addressed by a text.

**Worked example:** Passage: The trial compared two watering schedules in spring. Weekly watering used less water. A claim that daily watering used less is contradicted; a claim about winter watering is not addressed.

**Practice:** Passage: A bus study counted morning passengers at two stations in April. The west station had more passengers. Classify: (a) The east station was busier in the morning. (b) The researchers measured waiting times.

**Answer / success criteria:** (a) False: the text states the opposite about morning passengers. (b) Not Given: it does not state whether waiting times were measured.

## LAN-05 · Find the right rule in a notice

**Audience:** IELTS General Training Reading

**Objective:** Scan a notice for the location, deadline and applicable exception.

**Worked example:** Notice: Desk B accepts returns until 17:00. Outside those hours, books may go in the drop box; equipment must be returned to staff. A book at 17:15 goes in the box, but a charger does not.

**Practice:** Notice: Room keys are collected at reception before 16:30. After 16:30, pre-booked visitors collect keys from the caretaker; visitors without a booking must return on the next working day. Where should a booked visitor go at 17:00? What about a walk-in?

**Answer / success criteria:** The booked visitor goes to the caretaker. A walk-in must return on the next working day.

## LAN-06 · Listen past a corrected detail

**Audience:** IELTS Academic and General Training Listening

**Objective:** Note the final confirmed location and time rather than the first suggestion.

**Worked example:** Tutor reads: “We usually meet in Room Two. This week it's Room Four, and the group starts at half past two.” The learner writes Room Four and 2:30.

**Practice:** Tutor reads aloud without showing this text: “Your collection was booked for Wednesday at ten, but that slot has closed. I've moved it to Thursday at eleven, at the side entrance.” Learner records day, time and entrance.

**Answer / success criteria:** Thursday; 11:00; side entrance. Wednesday at ten was the superseded booking.

## LAN-07 · Tell a short story in sequence

**Audience:** IELTS Academic and General Training Speaking

**Objective:** Use setting, problem, response and result to keep a personal account easy to follow.

**Worked example:** Model: “Last autumn I got lost on my way to a club. My phone had run out of power, so I asked a shopkeeper for directions. I arrived ten minutes late, but the group welcomed me.”

**Practice:** Speak for 45–60 seconds about a time you helped someone find a place. Mention where it happened, what you did and the outcome.

**Answer / success criteria:** Sample opening: “On Monday, a visitor at my college was looking for the library. I showed her the lift and walked with her to the second floor. She found the right desk and thanked me.” Check setting, action and result.

## LAN-08 · Qualify a general opinion

**Audience:** IELTS Academic and General Training Speaking

**Objective:** Give an opinion, one reason and a circumstance that might change it.

**Worked example:** “I generally prefer printed maps because the whole route is visible. However, if the route changes frequently, a live map is more useful.”

**Practice:** Answer aloud: Should local classes always be offered online? Add a reason and a meaningful exception.

**Answer / success criteria:** “Online access helps people who live far away. However, a woodworking course may need an in-person option because beginners benefit from direct supervision.” A different balanced answer is acceptable.

## LAN-09 · Confirming a surname

**Audience:** German everyday beginner

**Objective:** Give a fictional surname and spell out its letters when asked.

**Worked example:** „Wie lautet Ihr Nachname?“ „Mein Nachname ist Falk.“ „Können Sie das bitte buchstabieren?“ „Ja: F, A, L, K.“

**Practice:** Role-play a library registration under the fictional surname Wolf. State it and spell its four letters. Partner checks the spelling.

**Answer / success criteria:** „Mein Nachname ist Wolf. W, O, L, F.“ The partner should recognise all four letters in order.

## LAN-10 · Rescheduling an appointment

**Audience:** German everyday beginner

**Objective:** Say when you cannot come and ask for an alternative time.

**Worked example:** „Am Mittwoch kann ich nicht. Geht es am Donnerstag um zehn Uhr?“ The verb kann stays second after Am Mittwoch.

**Practice:** Call about a neighbourhood course. You cannot come on Friday; suggest Monday at two in the afternoon.

**Answer / success criteria:** „Am Freitag kann ich nicht. Geht es am Montag um vierzehn Uhr?“ Equivalent: „… am Montag um zwei Uhr nachmittags?“

## LAN-11 · Understanding directions

**Audience:** German everyday beginner

**Objective:** Use straight ahead and left or right with one destination landmark.

**Worked example:** „Gehen Sie geradeaus. Biegen Sie an der Ampel rechts ab. Die Post ist neben der Apotheke.“ Geradeaus = straight ahead; neben = next to.

**Practice:** Ask where the library is. Then give the route: go straight, turn left at the traffic light, library beside the bank.

**Answer / success criteria:** „Entschuldigung, wo ist die Bibliothek?“ „Gehen Sie geradeaus und biegen Sie an der Ampel links ab. Die Bibliothek ist neben der Bank.“

## LAN-12 · Reporting a household problem

**Audience:** German everyday beginner

**Objective:** Describe a problem and politely ask when someone can come.

**Worked example:** „Die Heizung funktioniert nicht. Können Sie bitte jemanden schicken?“ Use funktioniert nicht for “isn't working”.

**Practice:** Write a two-sentence message to building maintenance: the front-door bell isn't working; ask when someone can come.

**Answer / success criteria:** „Die Klingel an der Haustür funktioniert nicht. Wann kann jemand vorbeikommen?“

## LAN-13 · Asking for an accessible route

**Audience:** French everyday beginner

**Objective:** Ask whether there is a lift and where to find it.

**Worked example:** « Y a-t-il un ascenseur ? » « Oui, il est à gauche, près de l’entrée. » Use près de for “near”.

**Practice:** At a fictional community centre, ask if there is a lift and whether it is near reception.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Excusez-moi, y a-t-il un ascenseur ? Est-il près de l’accueil ? »

## LAN-14 · Confirming the day and time

**Audience:** French everyday beginner

**Objective:** Repeat back a proposed weekday and time to avoid a misunderstanding.

**Worked example:** « Le cours est jeudi à quinze heures. » « D’accord, jeudi à quinze heures, c’est bien ça ? » Quinze heures is 3 p.m.

**Practice:** Your tutor says « Le cours est mardi à dix-sept heures. » Repeat and ask for confirmation; give the time in English.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Mardi à dix-sept heures, c’est bien ça ? » Tuesday at 5 p.m.

## LAN-15 · Making a polite repair request

**Audience:** French everyday beginner

**Objective:** Name the defective item and request help using pourriez-vous.

**Worked example:** « La sonnette ne fonctionne plus. Pourriez-vous venir la réparer ? » La refers to la sonnette.

**Practice:** Write to maintenance: the kitchen tap is leaking; ask if someone can look at it this week.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Le robinet de la cuisine fuit. Pourriez-vous venir voir cette semaine ? »

## LAN-16 · Asking about prices and payment

**Audience:** French everyday beginner

**Objective:** Ask the price and whether card payment is accepted.

**Worked example:** « Combien ça coûte ? » « Dix euros. » « Est-ce que je peux payer par carte ? »

**Practice:** Ask the price of two notebooks together and whether you can pay in cash.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Combien coûtent les deux cahiers ? Est-ce que je peux payer en espèces ? »

## LAN-17 · Checking course availability

**Audience:** Italian everyday beginner

**Objective:** Ask whether two places are available and for the start date.

**Worked example:** « Ci sono ancora due posti per il corso di cucina? Quando comincia il corso? » Ci sono asks whether plural items exist.

**Practice:** Ask if two places remain in the Italian course and when the first lesson is.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Ci sono ancora due posti per il corso di italiano? Quando c’è la prima lezione? »

## LAN-18 · Clarification at a service desk

**Audience:** Italian everyday beginner

**Objective:** Request slower speech and repeat the understood destination.

**Worked example:** « Può ripetere più lentamente, per favore? » « Il modulo va consegnato allo sportello tre. » « Quindi allo sportello tre, giusto? »

**Practice:** You heard that a form goes to counter five. Ask the clerk to repeat more slowly and then check the number.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Può ripetere più lentamente, per favore? Quindi allo sportello cinque, giusto? »

## LAN-19 · Asking about a bus stop

**Audience:** Italian everyday beginner

**Objective:** Identify the destination and ask where to get off.

**Worked example:** « Questo autobus va alla stazione? A quale fermata devo scendere? » Alla combines a + la.

**Practice:** Ask whether this bus goes to the library and at which stop to get off.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Questo autobus va alla biblioteca? A quale fermata devo scendere? »

## LAN-20 · Coordinating a partner’s arrival

**Audience:** Italian everyday beginner

**Objective:** Give an estimated arrival time and suggest a meeting spot.

**Worked example:** « Mia moglie arriva alle sei. Possiamo aspettarla davanti all’ingresso. » Use davanti a for “in front of”.

**Practice:** Say your husband arrives at seven and suggest waiting for him outside the station.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Mio marito arriva alle sette. Possiamo aspettarlo fuori dalla stazione. »

## LAN-21 · Finding the right form desk

**Audience:** Spanish everyday beginner

**Objective:** Ask where to collect a form and confirm the desk number.

**Worked example:** « ¿Dónde puedo recoger el formulario? » « En la ventanilla dos. » « ¿En la ventanilla dos, verdad? »

**Practice:** Ask where to collect a fictional library card form. The clerk says ventanilla cuatro; confirm aloud.

**Answer / success criteria:** « ¿Dónde puedo recoger el formulario para el carné de la biblioteca? ¿En la ventanilla cuatro, verdad? »

## LAN-22 · Asking if a partner may join

**Audience:** Spanish everyday beginner

**Objective:** Ask a direct registration question and check whether separate forms are needed.

**Worked example:** « ¿Puede venir mi pareja conmigo al curso? ¿Necesitamos dos formularios? » Conmigo means “with me”.

**Practice:** A neighbour invites you to a free photography workshop. Ask if your partner can come too and if you need to register separately.

**Answer / success criteria:** « ¿Puede venir mi pareja también? ¿Tenemos que inscribirnos por separado? »

## LAN-23 · Changing a collection time

**Audience:** Spanish everyday beginner

**Objective:** Turn down one time politely and propose another.

**Worked example:** « Lo siento, el jueves a las cuatro no puedo. ¿Puede ser a las seis? » Puede ser is a useful polite alternative proposal.

**Practice:** You cannot collect a package on Wednesday at ten. Propose Friday at eleven.

**Answer / success criteria:** « Lo siento, el miércoles a las diez no puedo. ¿Puede ser el viernes a las once? »

## LAN-24 · Following a service notice

**Audience:** Spanish everyday beginner

**Objective:** Identify whom a notice applies to, what to bring and when.

**Worked example:** « Recogida de material: martes de 9 a 12. Traiga el recibo. » A person collecting an item can go Tuesday between 9 and noon and should bring the receipt.

**Practice:** Read « Taller de dibujo: viernes de 16 a 18. Solo para personas inscritas. Traiga un lápiz. » State the day, time, who can attend and what they should bring.

**Answer / success criteria:** Friday, 4–6 p.m.; only people who are registered; bring a pencil. In Spanish: « El viernes de cuatro a seis. Solo para personas inscritas. Hay que llevar un lápiz. »
