# Vocational Levels 2–6: 24 items

**Staff review only.** Suggested difficulty only. Check the exact qualification and unit before use; no credit or assessment sign-off. Care examples are fictional, nonclinical teaching exercises. Use synthetic information.

## VOC-01 · Check a customer order

**Audience:** Level 2 / Business

**Objective:** Read back a quantity, price and collection slot before confirming an order.

**Worked example:** A fictional stationery shop receives “three A4 packs at £4 each, Friday at 3 pm.” The assistant reads back three packs, £12 total and Friday at 3 pm; the customer confirms all three details.

**Practice:** Confirm an order of four notebooks at £2.50 each for Tuesday at 11 am. What do you do if the customer says “Wednesday, not Tuesday”?

**Answer / success criteria:** Read back four notebooks, £10 total, Wednesday at 11 am after correcting the request; ask for confirmation before recording it.

## VOC-02 · Offer a meaningful choice

**Audience:** Level 2 / Health and social care

**Objective:** Use two manageable options and allow time for a person to answer.

**Worked example:** In a fictional day centre, Sam prefers to choose activities. A worker says “Would you like painting, the garden, or neither?” and pauses instead of treating silence as agreement.

**Practice:** Role-play inviting Jo to choose between music and a puzzle. Jo does not answer immediately. Demonstrate a suitable next line.

**Answer / success criteria:** Ask once and pause. If Jo still does not answer, offer to return later or ask whether another way of communicating would help. Do not record agreement.

## VOC-03 · Build a petty cash record

**Audience:** Level 2 / Accounting

**Objective:** Record two cash payments and reconcile the resulting balance.

**Worked example:** The fictional tiling group starts with £50 petty cash. Receipted payments of £8.30 and £6.20 leave £35.50. The recorded balance should match the cash counted.

**Practice:** Start with £40. Enter payments of £4.60 and £11.25. Counted cash is £23.15. Find the difference and name one useful check.

**Answer / success criteria:** Recorded cash is £24.15, so counted cash is £1 short. Check vouchers and recount before documenting and reporting an unexplained difference.

## VOC-04 · Clean a stock list

**Audience:** Level 2 / Technology

**Objective:** Apply consistent spreadsheet formatting without changing the meaning of records.

**Worked example:** A practice sheet has SKU 0041, item “Bolt ” and quantity 5. Format its SKU as text before entry to preserve leading zeroes, trim the item's extra space and keep quantity numeric.

**Practice:** Clean SKU 0072, item “ Washer ” and quantity entered as the text “12”. Explain why SKU and quantity should have different types.

**Answer / success criteria:** Store SKU as text “0072”, item as “Washer” and quantity as the number 12. A SKU identifies; quantity is used in arithmetic.

## VOC-05 · Compare supplier quotations

**Audience:** Level 3 / Business

**Objective:** Compare total costs and judge whether each option meets a delivery constraint.

**Worked example:** Supplier A offers 40 folders at £2.30 plus £12 delivery in two days, £104 total. B offers £2.50 each with free delivery in four days, £100. If stock is needed in three days, A meets the deadline and B does not.

**Practice:** For 60 folders, A charges £1.80 each plus £15 in two days; B charges £2.10 each delivered tomorrow. You need them tomorrow. Show both totals and choose.

**Answer / success criteria:** A totals £123 but misses the deadline; B totals £126 and meets it. Confirm B’s stock and delivery commitment before ordering.

## VOC-06 · Separate observation from interpretation

**Audience:** Level 3 / Health and social care

**Objective:** Rewrite a fictional handover using observable facts and the person’s own words.

**Worked example:** Replace “Rae was difficult” with “At 10:15, Rae moved away from the table and said ‘It is too noisy here.’ Staff offered a quieter seat; Rae declined.” The revision leaves out judgment.

**Practice:** Rewrite “Mina was lazy and wouldn’t join” using these facts: 14:05, stayed in the lounge, said “I’d like to finish my book first”, agreed to be asked again at 14:30.

**Answer / success criteria:** “At 14:05, Mina remained in the lounge and said, ‘I’d like to finish my book first.’ Mina agreed to staff asking again at 14:30.” Keep only what is relevant for the agreed handover.

## VOC-07 · Reconcile timing differences

**Audience:** Level 3 / Accounting

**Objective:** Reconcile a cash-book balance to a bank statement without treating timing items as errors.

**Worked example:** At month end the cash book shows £1,220. A £300 deposit is not yet on the statement, and a £75 payment recorded in the cash book has not cleared. Expected statement balance: £1,220 − £300 + £75 = £995.

**Practice:** Cash-book balance is £2,040, with £450 deposits in transit and £110 outstanding payments. The statement reads £1,700. Reconcile and state a follow-up check.

**Answer / success criteria:** £2,040 − £450 + £110 = £1,700. Check both timing items on the next statement and investigate any that do not clear as expected.

## VOC-08 · Design boundary tests

**Audience:** Level 3 / Technology

**Objective:** Select tests just below, at and just above permitted values.

**Worked example:** A fictional booking form accepts whole group sizes 2 through 8. Use 1→reject, 2→accept, 3→accept, 7→accept, 8→accept, 9→reject. A decimal also tests the whole-number requirement.

**Practice:** For permitted box weights of 5 to 12 whole kilograms, write six boundary cases with expected results and one data-type case.

**Answer / success criteria:** 4 reject, 5 accept, 6 accept, 11 accept, 12 accept, 13 reject. For example 7.5 rejects because the field accepts whole numbers only.

## VOC-09 · Balance queue metrics

**Audience:** Level 4 / Business

**Objective:** Read a simple waiting-time dashboard without confusing throughput with the experience of each customer.

**Worked example:** In one hour, Hill Café serves 30 and its median wait is 6 minutes; Glen Café serves 36 but its median is 9. The busiest location is not automatically the fastest; check the share waiting over the service target as well.

**Practice:** Site C serves 45 per hour with median wait 7 minutes and 8 of 45 over 12 minutes; Site D serves 35 with median 8 minutes and 2 of 35 over 12. What trade-off appears?

**Answer / success criteria:** C serves more and has a shorter median, but about 17.8% exceed 12 minutes versus about 5.7% at D. Investigate C’s long waits and compare like-for-like periods before changing staffing.

## VOC-10 · Audit an accessible activity

**Audience:** Level 4 / Health and social care

**Objective:** Design a small review that compares experience with attendance while respecting autonomy.

**Worked example:** A fictional drop-in records attendance of 12, including repeat visits. Ask participants privately, and only if they want to respond, which information format helped them choose. Record unique attendance separately from repeat visits and do not infer preferences from attendance alone.

**Practice:** Build a two-week review for a craft session moving from an upstairs room to ground floor. Propose two measures and one limitation.

**Answer / success criteria:** Compare visits and unique participants by session, and collect optional anonymous feedback on getting into and using the room. Different weather or staff may explain some change; attendance cannot show why people stayed away.

## VOC-11 · Build a flexible cost budget

**Audience:** Level 4 / Accounting

**Objective:** Separate the volume effect from spending against the expected cost for actual activity.

**Worked example:** Budget for 100 packs uses £4 variable each and £150 fixed, £550. At 120 packs flexed cost is £630. Actual £650 is £20 adverse against the flexed budget, while the extra £80 budget cost came from volume.

**Practice:** Plan 80 kits at £6 variable each plus £200 fixed. Make 90 for £760. Calculate original budget, flexed budget and variance against flexed cost.

**Answer / success criteria:** Original £680; flexed £740; actual £760, £20 adverse against flexed cost. £60 of the £80 difference from original budget is expected from higher volume.

## VOC-12 · Model a booking database

**Audience:** Level 4 / Technology

**Objective:** Choose keys and relationships that allow repeated bookings without duplicating customer information.

**Worked example:** A fictional workshop database stores Customer(customer_id, display_name) and Booking(booking_id, customer_id, start_time). Each booking has its own key; customer_id in Booking references Customer; the relationship is one-to-many.

**Practice:** A learner can submit several practice tasks, and each task can receive work from several learners. Propose three tables, and say how to allow at most one submission per pair.

**Answer / success criteria:** Learner(learner_id), Task(task_id), Submission(learner_id, task_id, submitted_at); use a composite primary key on Submission’s two foreign keys, or a separate primary key plus a unique constraint on the pair.

## VOC-13 · Evaluate a capacity investment

**Audience:** Level 5 / Business

**Objective:** Use contribution and demand uncertainty to state an equipment decision.

**Worked example:** A fictional print club can lease equipment for £900. Each extra order contributes £6 after added variable costs. Break-even 150 incremental orders; a cautious forecast of 130 produces a £120 shortfall, so trial demand before leasing.

**Practice:** Lease a cutter for £1,200; incremental contribution is £8 per job. Forecast is 140–185 extra jobs. Calculate break-even and both forecast outcomes, then choose a next step.

**Answer / success criteria:** Break-even is 150 jobs. At 140: £80 shortfall; at 185: £280 surplus. Validate orders via a reversible pilot or rental before a fixed commitment.

## VOC-14 · Test an improvement without overclaiming

**Audience:** Level 5 / Health and social care

**Objective:** Design a small service-improvement pilot with a balancing measure.

**Worked example:** A fictional service tries a plain-language timetable for two weeks. Main measure: percentage of consenting respondents who can identify the next activity. Balancing measure: staff time to maintain both formats. Small response counts limit generalisation.

**Practice:** Design a one-week test of a co-designed reminder card for optional groups. Specify how to collect feedback, an outcome, a balancing measure and a limitation.

**Answer / success criteria:** Offer the card to willing participants, invite anonymous feedback, compare the share who can identify the next group time, and log time staff spend updating cards. Small samples and changes in group popularity make causal claims unsafe.

## VOC-15 · Discount alternative cash flows

**Audience:** Level 5 / Accounting

**Objective:** Compare two feasible projects using net present value at a stated fictional rate.

**Worked example:** Two-year project costs £1,000 and returns £600 each year. At 10% the NPV is £600/1.10 + £600/1.10² − £1,000 ≈ £41.32; a positive NPV depends on the cash-flow and rate assumptions.

**Practice:** At a teaching rate of 10%, compare projects each costing £1,000. X returns £770 then £440; Y returns £440 then £847. Calculate both NPVs and select by NPV alone.

**Answer / success criteria:** X: £700 + £363.64 − £1,000 ≈ £63.64. Y: £400 + £700 − £1,000 = £100. Choose Y on these assumptions, then test sensitivity before a real decision.

## VOC-16 · Plan a controlled release

**Audience:** Level 5 / Technology

**Objective:** Define monitoring signals and a rollback trigger before a fictional app release.

**Worked example:** For a booking change on a synthetic test system, compare success and latency against the previous build. If booking failures exceed 2% over a suitable sample, stop the rollout and use the rehearsed revert path; check recovery before further rollout.

**Practice:** Write a staged-release plan for a fictional timetable export change. Specify cohort, measures, rollback threshold and validation after rollback.

**Answer / success criteria:** Begin with an internal test cohort using synthetic schedules. Check successful exports and correct rows/time zones. Stop for any confirmed data correctness problem; revert to the previous export version and rerun known schedule cases to confirm recovery.

## VOC-17 · Interpret a pilot under uncertainty

**Audience:** Level 6 / Business

**Objective:** Distinguish a descriptive change from causal evidence and recommend a proportionate test.

**Worked example:** A fictional firm trial sees 32/40 buyers reorder versus 22/30 in an older cohort, 80% versus about 73%. Cohort timing and composition differ; recommend a comparative pilot rather than crediting the policy with a seven-point gain.

**Practice:** A self-selected new-service group renews at 54/60, compared with 76/100 for the conventional group. Quantify the difference, identify a confounder and recommend a design.

**Answer / success criteria:** 90% vs 76% gives 14 percentage points. Willing early adopters may be more engaged. If feasible and appropriate, compare concurrently using random assignment, agreed eligibility and an outcome chosen before observing results.

## VOC-18 · Judge representativeness in feedback

**Audience:** Level 6 / Health and social care

**Objective:** Explain why a higher response score may not indicate a service-wide improvement.

**Worked example:** An opt-in survey finds 18 of 20 positive responses after switching to online-only delivery. The previous mixed-format survey had 34 of 50 positives. Better responses may reflect who can access the survey rather than better care.

**Practice:** During a fictional service review, 27/30 online respondents and 12/20 telephone respondents report clear information. Calculate each proportion and recommend how to reduce access bias.

**Answer / success criteria:** Online 90%; telephone 60%; combined respondents 39/50 = 78%. Report both samples, offer verbal, written and accessible-format routes, and avoid inferring outcomes for nonresponders.

## VOC-19 · Find a project’s sensitivity threshold

**Audience:** Level 6 / Accounting

**Objective:** Calculate the cash flow at which a project would have zero net present value.

**Worked example:** A fictional one-year project costs £9,000. With a 12% discount rate it needs £10,080 in year-one cash flow to break even. A £10,500 forecast has £420 in nominal cash-flow headroom, not £420 NPV.

**Practice:** A one-year project costs £15,000 and projects £17,500 at a 10% rate. Calculate break-even cash flow, the allowable decline as a percentage of forecast, and current NPV.

**Answer / success criteria:** Break-even is £16,500. The £1,000 cushion is 5.71% of forecast. NPV is £17,500/1.10 − £15,000 ≈ £909.09. Recheck the conclusion if the rate or timing changes.

## VOC-20 · Decide a retention design

**Audience:** Level 6 / Technology

**Objective:** Compare raw and aggregate storage against operational needs while minimising collected data.

**Worked example:** The team wants monthly counts and a short troubleshooting window. A fictional proposal keeps events briefly with a random session identifier, then retains aggregate counts only. Document expiry, responsibility, and what cannot be investigated after deletion.

**Practice:** For a classroom scheduling app that must show term totals and investigate export failures for seven days, propose fields and tests. No real user data is needed.

**Answer / success criteria:** Use synthetic event time, outcome, export type and anonymous trace ID, without names or schedule text. Keep troubleshooting events for the stated seven-day teaching scenario and aggregate by term. Test expiry, repeat aggregation and whether trace review answers the required question without storing more.

## VOC-21 · Test a stock reorder rule

**Audience:** Level 4 / Business

**Objective:** Apply a simple reorder level and recognise how a fixed rule fails under variability.

**Worked example:** In a fictional stationery shop, demand is 12 packs per day, replenishment takes 4 days and buffer is 15. Reorder point is 12×4 + 15 = 63 packs, compared with inventory position including incoming orders and unmet demand.

**Practice:** Daily use is 8, lead time is 5 days and buffer is 12. On hand 40, 18 on order, 7 backordered. Should the example rule trigger?

**Answer / success criteria:** Reorder point is 52; inventory position is 40 + 18 − 7 = 51, so trigger. Review the assumptions if lead time or usage changes.

## VOC-22 · Make a service change with people

**Audience:** Level 5 / Health and social care

**Objective:** Identify where service users can influence a proposal beyond a one-off survey.

**Worked example:** A fictional centre sets two feasible opening-time options and invites an accessible workshop before deciding. Staff record what changed in response to feedback and explain constraints so consultation is not presented as a guarantee.

**Practice:** Plan a co-design step for a new reception sign. Include a way to involve people who dislike group discussion and a way to show decisions.

**Answer / success criteria:** Offer short one-to-one or anonymous feedback alongside a small group; ask participants to try locating the room with draft signs; share the chosen wording and list which suggestions were used and why others were not.

## VOC-23 · Track cash versus credit

**Audience:** Level 3 / Accounting

**Objective:** Separate cash receipts from credit sales and identify the resulting outstanding amount.

**Worked example:** On a fictional day, sales are £120 cash and £180 on credit. Sales total £300; today's cash received from those sales is £120; £180 remains owed, assuming no other events.

**Practice:** A café sells £70 for cash and £230 on credit. It also receives £40 on a prior invoice. Separate today's revenue, total cash receipts and new receivables.

**Answer / success criteria:** Revenue is £300; total cash receipts are £110; newly raised receivables are £230. The £40 reduces earlier receivables and is not new revenue.

## VOC-24 · Diagnose a spreadsheet total

**Audience:** Level 2 / Technology

**Objective:** Use a small expected result to diagnose a missing spreadsheet cell.

**Worked example:** A delivery sheet records 8, 10 and 12 items in B2:B4. SUM(B2:B3) returns 18 instead of 30 because B4 is missing. Make the range match the three intended rows.

**Practice:** A sheet has 9, 7, 4 in B2:B4. SUM(B3:B4) returns 11. Correct the formula and propose a quick regression check.

**Answer / success criteria:** Use =SUM(B2:B4), expecting 20. Change B2 from 9 to 10 temporarily and confirm the total becomes 21; then restore the practice data.
